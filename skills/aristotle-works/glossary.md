# Glossary

Definitions below are interpretive paraphrases of the listed source locations, not quotations.

**Activity** — An exercise or actualization of a capacity; central to happiness and pleasure (Ethics I, X).

**Affection** — Undergoing or being acted upon; one of the ten categories (Categories, Part 4).

**Art / craft** — A reasoned productive capacity concerned with making, contrasted with action (Ethics VI).

**Catharsis** — A disputed technical term in the account of tragedy's effect through pity and fear; compare translations before relying on one rendering (Poetics, ch. VI; Art of Poetry, tragedy definition).

**Character friendship** — Reciprocal friendship grounded in appreciation of each person's character and good, rather than utility or pleasure alone (Ethics VIII–IX).

**Choice** — Deliberate desire concerning things within one's power; narrower than wish or appetite (Ethics III).

**Citizen** — In the core political definition, one who shares in deliberative or judicial office; historically restricted in Aristotle's account (Politics III).

**Cleverness** — Capacity to find effective means to a target; becomes practical wisdom only when ordered to a good end (Ethics VI).

**Constitution** — The ordering of offices and authoritative power in a city, including the end pursued by the association (Politics III–IV).

**Corrective justice** — Equality-restoring correction in transactions or injuries (Ethics V).

**Deliberation** — Inquiry about achievable means in matters that can be otherwise and depend on action (Ethics III, VI).

**Distributive justice** — Allocation of common goods according to an asserted proportional basis (Ethics V).

**End** — That for the sake of which action is chosen; ends may be subordinate or final (Ethics I).

**Equity** — Context-sensitive correction of a general legal rule where its wording misses the relevant particular (Ethics V).

**Equivocal** — Sharing a name while differing in the definition associated with that name (Categories, Part 1).

**Flourishing / happiness** — The final human good analyzed as excellent activity across a complete life, with enabling conditions (Ethics I, X).

**Habituation** — Formation of stable dispositions through repeated action and responses of pleasure and pain (Ethics II; Politics VIII).

**Imitation** — Representation differentiated by medium, object, and manner in the poetic arts (Poetics, chs. I–III).

**Justice** — Broadly, lawfulness and complete virtue toward others; particularly, fairness in distribution and correction (Ethics V).

**Mean** — The context-sensitive appropriate response between excess and deficiency, relative to the agent and determined by reason (Ethics II).

**Polity** — A mixed constitutional form oriented toward common advantage, often associated with a substantial middle element (Politics III–IV).

**Practical wisdom** — Truth-attaining rational excellence concerning good action in contingent particulars (Ethics VI).

**Primary substance** — An individual underlying subject, neither said of nor present in another subject in the relevant senses (Categories, Parts 2, 5).

**Probability and necessity** — Relations by which incidents should follow in a unified plot (Poetics, chs. VII–XI).

**Reversal** — A change in the direction of action that arises from the plot's causal structure (Poetics, chs. X–XI).

**Recognition** — A change from ignorance to knowledge, strongest when integrated with reversal and plot (Poetics, chs. XI, XVI).

**Self-control** — A state in which appetite conflicts with reason but the agent follows the better judgment (Ethics VII).

**Substance** — The primary category of underlying individual things and the species or genera predicated of them (Categories, Part 5).

**Univocal** — Sharing both a name and the corresponding definition (Categories, Part 1).

**Voluntary action** — Action whose source is in the agent with relevant knowledge of particulars; assessed in context (Ethics III).

**Weakness / lack of self-control** — Failure to act on better judgment under appetite or emotion, distinguished from settled vice (Ethics VII).
