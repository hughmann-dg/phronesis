# Aristotle Works — Decision Cheatsheet

Use this as an index to Aristotle's arguments, not as authority that overrides evidence, rights, or modern ethics.

## Fast Routing

| If the question is about… | Start with | Then check |
|---|---|---|
| Ultimate aims and flourishing | Ethics I | Ethics X; Politics VII |
| Habit, character, excess, deficiency | Ethics II | Ethics IV; Politics VIII |
| Responsibility and constrained choice | Ethics III | Ethics VII |
| Fair distribution or remedy | Ethics V | Politics III; Athenian Constitution |
| Judgment under uncertainty and particulars | Ethics VI | Ethics III; Politics IV |
| Weakness against better judgment | Ethics VII | Ethics III, X |
| Friendship, trust, and reciprocity | Ethics VIII–IX | Ethics V |
| Definition or ambiguous language | Categories | Ethics V; Politics III |
| Narrative structure | Poetics—Butcher | Poetics—Bywater comparison |
| Citizenship and regime types | Politics III | Politics IV–VI |
| Feasible institutional design | Politics IV | Politics V–VI |
| Regime failure and faction | Politics V | Athenian Constitution 13–41 |
| Offices, courts, and accountability | Athenian Constitution 42–69 | Politics VI |
| Comparative biological observation | History of Animals I–IX | Modern scientific sources |

## Practical-Wisdom Flow

1. **End** — What is worth pursuing for its own sake? (Ethics I)
2. **Agency** — What is genuinely within our power here? (Ethics III)
3. **Particulars** — Which facts change what fitting action means? (Ethics VI)
4. **Character** — What disposition will this choice express and reinforce? (Ethics II)
5. **Proportion** — What would excess and deficiency look like in this case? (Ethics II, IV)
6. **Justice** — Is this allocation, correction, exchange, or a case for equity? (Ethics V)
7. **Relationships** — What expectations, reciprocity, or trust does it alter? (Ethics VIII–IX)
8. **Disconfirmation** — What evidence or expert knowledge should overrule this lens?

## Institutional Smells

| Smell | Diagnostic question | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Regime label hides power | Who actually selects, pays, audits, judges, and removes? | Politics III–VI |
| “Equal” without a basis | Equal in what respect, for what distribution? | Ethics V; Politics V |
| Ideal ignores conditions | What is best feasible from the present state? | Politics IV.1 |
| Gradual capture | Which small rule changes accumulate advantage unnoticed? | Politics V |
| Unity suppresses plurality | Has coordination become domination or erased necessary differences? | Politics II |
| Education as private afterthought | Which civic habits does the system reproduce? | Politics VIII |

## Narrative Smells

- An incident can be removed without changing the whole → weak unity (Poetics VIII).
- Surprise has no causal preparation → arbitrary reversal or recognition (Poetics X–XI).
- Spectacle carries an effect the action does not earn → structural weakness (Poetics XIV).
- One English term bears doctrinal weight → compare Butcher and Bywater translations.

## Hard Stops

- Do not operationalize Aristotle's defenses of slavery, sex hierarchy, or restricted citizenship.
- Do not use *History of Animals* for medical, veterinary, or scientific conclusions without modern verification.
- Treat *History of Animals* Book X as doubtfully attributed.
- Do not present translator introductions or prefaces as Aristotle's claims.
- Do not present a paraphrase as a quotation.
