# Politics — Book VII

## Core Idea

The best constitution depends on an account of the best life, then on material, demographic, spatial, civic, and educational conditions capable of supporting that life.

## Reference Map

- Best life, goods of soul, and relation between individual and civic flourishing (chs. 1–3).
- Scale, territory, location, and population (chs. 4–7).
- Necessary functions, social roles, citizenship, and property (chs. 8–10).
- Urban planning, defense, common meals, worship, and public space (chs. 11–12).
- End, leisure, peace, character, and stages of education (chs. 13–17).

## Decision Rules

- Define the life the polity should enable before optimizing institutions or resources.
- Treat size as a functional question: large enough for self-sufficiency, bounded enough for order and knowledge.
- Design for peace and worthy leisure, not merely for victory or production.
- Distinguish necessary conditions from the end they support.

## Critical Limits

- Exclusionary assumptions about labor, citizenship, and social rank are historically situated and unacceptable as policy guidance.
