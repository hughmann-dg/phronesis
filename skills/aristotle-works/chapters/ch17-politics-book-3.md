# Politics — Book III

## Core Idea

Political analysis begins with citizenship and the constitution: who shares in deliberative and judicial office, whose interest rule serves, and what competing claims to justice justify authority.

## Reference Map

- Definition of citizen through participation in judgment and office (chs. 1–3).
- Good citizen and good person; civic virtue relative to constitution (chs. 4–5).
- Correct and deviant constitutions classified by rulers and common versus private interest (chs. 6–8).
- Claims of rich, free, noble, numerous, and virtuous groups (chs. 9–13).
- Kingship, law, and exceptional virtue (chs. 14–18).

## Decision Rules

- Define membership by actual powers and responsibilities, not residence or label alone.
- Classify regimes by both who rules and for whose advantage they rule.
- When rival groups claim justice, identify the partial good each captures and what it omits.
- Prefer law where general impartiality is possible, while recognizing that law still needs judgment in particulars.

## Critical Limits

- Citizenship definitions exclude many people on status grounds; use the analytical questions without importing those exclusions.
