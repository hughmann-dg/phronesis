# Nicomachean Ethics — Book II

## Core Idea

Moral excellence is formed through habituated action and consists in choosing the context-sensitive mean relative to us, as determined by reason and practical judgment.

## Reference Map

- Moral versus intellectual excellence and the role of habituation (ch. 1).
- Practice precedes stable character; pleasure and pain reveal formation (chs. 2–3).
- Conditions for acting excellently: knowledge, choice for its own sake, and stable character (ch. 4).
- Excellence as a state concerned with choice (chs. 5–6).
- The mean across specific emotions and actions (ch. 7).
- Practical ways to correct bias and move away from the nearer extreme (chs. 8–9).

## Decision Rules

- Judge training by the dispositions repeated action produces, not merely by stated values (chs. 1–2).
- Do not calculate the mean as an arithmetic midpoint; determine what fits the person and circumstances (ch. 6).
- Move first away from the extreme toward which one's own habits pull most strongly (ch. 9).
- Treat pleasure and distress as evidence about character formation (ch. 3).

## Anti-patterns

- Expecting correct theory alone to create good character.
- Applying “the mean” to actions Aristotle treats as wrong in themselves (ch. 6).
