# Nicomachean Ethics — Book VII

## Core Idea

The book investigates weakness and self-control, using reputable opinions and resolved puzzles to explain how appetite can conflict with knowledge and how pleasure relates to good activity.

## Reference Map

- Vice, lack of self-control, brutishness, and their contrasts (ch. 1).
- Method: preserve the strongest accepted appearances after resolving difficulties (ch. 1).
- Whether weakness can occur with knowledge and in what sense (chs. 2–5).
- Weakness concerning anger and bodily pleasures; weakness versus vice (chs. 6–10).
- Pleasure, pain, activity, and objections to pleasure as a good (chs. 11–14).

## Decision Rules

- Diagnose failure by asking whether the agent lacks knowledge, fails to activate it, or is overborne by appetite.
- Distinguish weakness from vice: the weak agent retains a better principle and experiences conflict; the vicious agent's settled end is distorted.
- When evaluating a theory, state the appearances and puzzles first, then preserve as many well-grounded judgments as the resolution permits.
- Do not treat every pleasure as good or every pleasure as bad; evaluate the activity and condition it completes.
