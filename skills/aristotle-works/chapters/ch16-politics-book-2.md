# Politics — Book II

## Core Idea

Proposed and existing constitutions should be tested against incentives, ownership, unity, faction, education, and actual institutional performance rather than admired only in theory.

## Reference Map

- Critique of community of property, spouses, and children in Plato's *Republic* (chs. 1–5).
- Critique of Plato's *Laws* and other proposed constitutions (chs. 6–8).
- Examination of Sparta, Crete, Carthage, and selected lawgivers (chs. 9–12).

## Decision Rules

- Do not maximize unity until a plural association ceases to function as a city (ch. 2).
- Test common ownership for diffusion of responsibility, conflict over effort, and loss of personal care (chs. 3–5).
- Compare institutional design with observed outcomes and failure modes, not stated ideals alone.
- Examine offices for selection, tenure, accountability, concentration, and vulnerability to purchase or favoritism.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating formal equality as sufficient while ignoring unequal contribution and incentives.
- Copying a prestigious constitution without considering population, economy, education, and civic habits.
