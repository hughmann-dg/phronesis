# History of Animals — Book IV

## Core Idea

Book IV turns to animals characterized as bloodless in the ancient scheme, then compares sensation, voice, sleep, lifespan, and sex differences across animals.

## Reference Map

- Cephalopods, crustaceans, testacea, echinoderms, ascidians, anemone-like animals, hermit crabs, and insects (chs. 1–7).
- Sense organs and sensation (ch. 8).
- Voice and other sound production (ch. 9).
- Sleep, age, and sexual differences (chs. 10–11).

## Inquiry Patterns

- When structures differ sharply, compare functions and modes of action rather than names alone.
- Use absence claims cautiously: failure to observe an organ is not proof it does not exist.
- Separate sound production from voice by specifying mechanism.

## Critical Limits

- “Bloodless” is a historical category, not a modern taxonomic clade.
- Many sensory and physiological claims are limited by ancient instruments.
