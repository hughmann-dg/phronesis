# Nicomachean Ethics — Book IV

## Core Idea

Virtue is expressed through proportionate conduct in resources, honor, anger, social interaction, truthfulness, humor, and appropriate shame.

## Reference Map

- Liberality and the use of wealth (chs. 1–3).
- Magnificence in large expenditure (chs. 4–6).
- Greatness of soul and proper relation to honor (chs. 7–9).
- Appropriate ambition and mildness concerning anger (chs. 10–11).
- Social agreeableness, truthfulness, and wit (chs. 12–14).
- Shame as a feeling more fitting to youth than a mature excellence (ch. 15).

## Decision Rules

- Evaluate giving by amount, recipient, timing, motive, and the giver's means—not by amount alone (chs. 1–3).
- Scale expenditure to purpose and circumstance; grandeur without proportion becomes vulgar display (chs. 4–6).
- Distinguish truthful self-presentation from boastfulness and self-deprecation (ch. 13).
- Calibrate anger to the right objects, manner, duration, and reason (ch. 11).

## Limits

- The social ideals and status assumptions in this book require historical and moral scrutiny before modern application.
