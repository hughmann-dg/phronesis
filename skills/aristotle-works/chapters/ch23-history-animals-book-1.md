# History of Animals — Book I

## Core Idea

Book I establishes comparative description by grouping animals through parts, modes of life, movement, reproduction, and habitat, then uses the familiar human body as a reference point.

## Reference Map

- Differences and likenesses in animal parts (chs. 1–4).
- Grouping by blood, generation, habitat, movement, and mode of life (chs. 5–6).
- Human external and internal parts as a comparative standard (chs. 7–14).

## Inquiry Patterns

- Begin with observable similarities and differences before proposing causes.
- Compare homologous parts, analogous functions, degrees, excesses, and defects.
- State the axes of classification explicitly; a single animal may belong to several cross-cutting groups.

## Critical Limits

- The taxonomy predates modern evolutionary systematics and contains observation errors.
- Human anatomy is used as a descriptive reference, not a valid hierarchy of species.
