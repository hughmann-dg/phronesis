# Nicomachean Ethics — Book I

## Core Idea

Action is organized by ends, and practical inquiry seeks the highest human end: flourishing understood as activity of soul in accordance with excellence across a complete life.

## Reference Map

- Ends and the architectonic role of political inquiry (chs. 1–3).
- Competing accounts of happiness and the need for a self-sufficient final end (chs. 4–7).
- The function argument and excellent rational activity (ch. 7).
- External goods, fortune, and the stability of flourishing (chs. 8–12).
- Rational and non-rational aspects of soul; transition to intellectual and moral excellence (ch. 13).

## Decision Rules

- Clarify which end is sought for itself and which aims are subordinate means (chs. 1–2).
- Match precision to the subject: practical matters admit variation and should not be forced into mathematical exactness (ch. 3).
- Evaluate a life through sustained activity and character, not a passing feeling or isolated success (chs. 7–10).
- Include enabling external conditions without confusing them with excellent activity itself (chs. 8–10).
