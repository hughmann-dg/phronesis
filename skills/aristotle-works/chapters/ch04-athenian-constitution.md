# The Athenian Constitution

## Core Idea

The work combines a historical account of constitutional change with a detailed description of Athenian institutions, showing how laws, offices, class conflict, and civic procedures shape a regime over time.

## Reference Map

- **Early order and social conflict**: offices, debt dependence, Draco, and the pre-Solonian crisis (Parts 1–7).
- **Solon's settlement**: property classes, laws, courts, and political consequences (Parts 7–13).
- **Tyranny and its fall**: Pisistratus and successors (Parts 14–20).
- **Cleisthenic and later reforms**: tribes, council, ostracism, and democratic development (Parts 21–29).
- **Oligarchic revolutions and restoration**: Four Hundred, Five Thousand, Thirty, and return of democracy (Parts 29–41).
- **Citizenship and training**: enrollment and ephebic service (Part 42).
- **Council and Assembly**: selection, responsibilities, agenda, and oversight (Parts 43–49).
- **Magistracies and administration**: financial, market, judicial, religious, and military offices (Parts 50–62).
- **Courts**: juror selection, allotment, panels, and voting procedure (Parts 63–69).

## Decision Rules

- Evaluate constitutions as evolving arrangements, not only as static legal texts.
- Track who selects, audits, pays, and removes officeholders; these procedures reveal practical power.
- Distinguish reform's stated aim from the coalition and institutional mechanism that sustain it.

## Limits

- The historical narrative reports contested events and inherited traditions; do not treat every causal attribution as independently verified.
- The text's final institutional description concerns its own period, not democratic government in general.
