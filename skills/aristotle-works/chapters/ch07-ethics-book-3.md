# Nicomachean Ethics — Book III

## Core Idea

Responsibility depends on voluntary action, choice, deliberation, and what lies within the agent's control; courage and temperance illustrate how choice shapes virtue.

## Reference Map

- Compulsion, ignorance, mixed actions, and voluntariness (chs. 1–3).
- Choice as distinct from appetite, spirit, wish, and opinion (ch. 4).
- Deliberation about means that are achievable through us (ch. 5).
- Wish, character, and responsibility for settled dispositions (chs. 6–7).
- Courage in relation to fear, confidence, and noble action (chs. 8–12).
- Temperance in bodily pleasures and appetites (chs. 13–15).

## Decision Rules

- Assess voluntariness at the time and under the particulars of action, especially in constrained choices (ch. 1).
- Deliberate about workable means, not fixed necessities or ends that action cannot affect (ch. 5).
- Separate genuine courage from confidence based on experience, passion, ignorance, or expected advantage (chs. 8–11).
- Include the formation of habits when assigning responsibility, while distinguishing compulsion and relevant ignorance (chs. 1–7).
