# Nicomachean Ethics — Book V

## Core Idea

Justice concerns lawful and fair relations, with distinct forms for distributing common goods, correcting transactions, and judging voluntary action under equitable law.

## Reference Map

- Justice and injustice in broad and particular senses (chs. 1–2).
- Distributive justice and proportional equality (ch. 3).
- Corrective justice in transactions and injuries (ch. 4).
- Exchange, reciprocity, money, and commensurability (ch. 5).
- Political justice, natural and legal aspects, and acts versus states (chs. 6–8).
- Questions about suffering injustice, self-injury, and responsibility (chs. 9–11).
- Equity as correction where general law misses a particular case (ch. 10 in common modern numbering; inspect edition headings when citing).

## Decision Rules

- Identify the kind of justice before choosing a remedy: distribution and correction use different proportional structures.
- Distinguish an unjust outcome, an unjust act, and an unjust character.
- Apply equity when a general rule's wording fails the case that its lawgiver would reasonably have addressed.
- Make the relevant basis of equality explicit; equal treatment without the right comparison class can remain unjust.
