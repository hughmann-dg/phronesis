# Nicomachean Ethics — Book VIII

## Core Idea

Friendship is necessary for life and civic association; its forms differ according to whether the relationship is based on utility, pleasure, or mutual appreciation of character.

## Reference Map

- Necessity, nobility, and civic importance of friendship (ch. 1).
- What is lovable and conditions of reciprocal recognized goodwill (chs. 2–3).
- Friendships of utility, pleasure, and character (chs. 3–6).
- Equality, proportionality, and unequal relationships (chs. 7–8).
- Friendship, justice, and forms of community and constitution (chs. 9–13).
- Complaints and exchange in different friendships (chs. 13–14).

## Decision Rules

- Identify what sustains a relationship: use, pleasure, or the other's character.
- Expect utility and pleasure relationships to dissolve when their basis changes; do not demand the stability of character friendship from them.
- In unequal relationships, seek proportionate reciprocity rather than identical return.
- Examine friendship and justice together because both vary with the kind of community.
