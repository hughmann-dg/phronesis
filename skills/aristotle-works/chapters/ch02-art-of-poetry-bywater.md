# On the Art of Poetry — Bywater Translation

## Core Idea

This edition offers a second English rendering of the *Poetics*, useful for checking how translation choices affect concepts such as imitation, action, probability, recognition, and catharsis.

## Reference Map

- **Imitative arts and their differences**: opening sections I–III.
- **Natural origins of poetry**: development from imitation and rhythm; differentiation of genres (opening discussion following III).
- **Constitution of tragedy**: action, plot, character, thought, diction, melody, and spectacle (central tragedy analysis).
- **Plot quality**: magnitude, unity, probability, necessity, reversal, recognition, and suffering (central sections).
- **Epic and criticism**: relation between epic and tragedy and replies to objections (closing sections I–III).

## Translation Use

- Consult this edition when a doctrinal claim depends on the wording of a technical term.
- Compare against Butcher before treating an English phrase as Aristotle's fixed formulation.
- Separate Gilbert Murray's preface from Aristotle's treatise; it is edition paratext, not primary Aristotelian argument.

## Decision Rules

- Trace an artistic effect to the structure of represented action before crediting style or spectacle.
- Ask whether each incident follows by probability or necessity from what precedes it.
- Use translation comparison to expose interpretive uncertainty rather than hiding it.
