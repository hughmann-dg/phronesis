# Politics — Book VI

## Core Idea

Different democracies and oligarchies require different institutional combinations, because population, livelihood, wealth, and participation shape which arrangements are workable and stable.

## Reference Map

- Varieties and combinations of democratic and oligarchic institutions (chs. 1–2).
- Equality, participation, and selection mechanisms (chs. 2–5).
- Agricultural, commercial, laboring, and military populations as regime conditions (chs. 4–7).
- Offices necessary for administration, markets, finance, law, defense, and religion (ch. 8).

## Decision Rules

- Match participation rules to the time, livelihood, and geographic distribution of citizens.
- Analyze institutions as bundles: assembly, office selection, courts, pay, eligibility, and tenure interact.
- Prefer arrangements that let groups participate without making public business a route to extraction.
- Define necessary offices by functions before deciding how each office is selected.

## Critical Limits

- Aristotle's restricted citizen body is not a modern democratic baseline; adapt institutional analysis only under equal civic standing.
