# Politics — Book V

## Core Idea

Constitutions change when groups generalize partial claims about equality, pursue advantage or honor, fear loss, or exploit institutional vulnerabilities; preservation requires treating regime-specific causes.

## Reference Map

- General causes of faction and constitutional change (chs. 1–4).
- Causes specific to democracies, oligarchies, aristocracies, and polities (chs. 5–7).
- Preservation measures across constitutions (chs. 8–9).
- Causes and preservation of monarchies and tyrannies (chs. 10–12).

## Decision Rules

- Trace conflict from the parties' own conceptions of equality and deserved share (ch. 1).
- Look for small initiating disputes that mobilize larger structural divisions (ch. 3).
- Preserve institutions by enforcing rules consistently, limiting accumulation of office and advantage, and preventing gradual unnoticed change (chs. 8–9).
- Distinguish advice about preserving a just constitution from descriptive advice about how domination perpetuates itself.

## Critical Limits

- The discussion of tyrannical preservation is diagnostic red-team material, not a recommendation for legitimate governance.
