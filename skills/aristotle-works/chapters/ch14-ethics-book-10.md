# Nicomachean Ethics — Book X

## Core Idea

Pleasure completes unimpeded activity, while the highest account of happiness emphasizes excellent activity—especially contemplation—and closes by turning from ethical argument to laws, education, and politics.

## Reference Map

- Competing views of pleasure and reasons to examine them (chs. 1–2).
- Pleasure as complete at each moment and as completing activity (chs. 3–5).
- Happiness as activity chosen for itself, not amusement (chs. 6–7).
- Contemplative and practically excellent lives (chs. 7–8).
- Need for habituation, law, and political science (ch. 9).

## Decision Rules

- Evaluate a pleasure partly by the activity it completes and the judge whose character is sound (chs. 4–5).
- Do not confuse recreation, which restores activity, with the final purpose of life (ch. 6).
- Keep ethical theory connected to formation: arguments alone rarely reform habits resistant to reason (ch. 9).
- When applying the hierarchy of lives, state its contested assumptions rather than presenting it as an unqualified modern conclusion.
