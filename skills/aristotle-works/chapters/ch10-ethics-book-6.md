# Nicomachean Ethics — Book VI

## Core Idea

Good action requires practical wisdom: a truth-attaining rational capacity concerned with achievable action and particulars, integrated with good character and sound deliberation.

## Reference Map

- Rational capacities for necessary and contingent matters (chs. 1–2).
- Scientific knowledge, art, practical wisdom, wisdom, and intuitive reason (chs. 3–7).
- Political and practical judgment at universal and particular levels (chs. 8–9).
- Deliberative excellence, understanding, judgment, and equitable discernment (chs. 10–11).
- Natural virtue, full virtue, cleverness, and the relation between character and practical wisdom (chs. 12–13).

## Decision Rules

- Do not reduce practical wisdom to knowledge of universals; action turns on particulars (chs. 7–8).
- Test deliberation by whether it reaches an appropriate end through correct means, not merely whether it is effective (ch. 9).
- Distinguish cleverness—the ability to reach a chosen target—from practical wisdom, which also requires a good target (ch. 12).
- Treat moral excellence and practical wisdom as mutually supporting: character sets the end, judgment identifies fitting action (chs. 12–13).

## Anti-patterns

- Applying a general principle without attending to the concrete case.
- Calling instrumental efficiency “wisdom” when the end is corrupt.
