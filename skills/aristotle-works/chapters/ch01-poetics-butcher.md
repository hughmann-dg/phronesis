# The Poetics — Butcher Translation

## Core Idea

Poetry is analyzed by its medium, object, and manner of imitation, with tragedy judged chiefly through the organization of action rather than spectacle or isolated verbal beauty.

## Reference Map

- **Kinds of imitation**: media, objects, and modes (chs. I–III).
- **Origins and genres**: development of poetry, tragedy, comedy, and epic (chs. IV–V).
- **Tragedy and its parts**: plot, character, thought, diction, song, and spectacle (ch. VI).
- **Plot construction**: wholeness, unity, probability, reversal, recognition, and suffering (chs. VII–XIV).
- **Character and recognition**: standards for characterization and kinds of discovery (chs. XV–XVI).
- **Composition practice**: visualize action, construct incidents, and manage episodes (chs. XVII–XVIII).
- **Language and epic**: thought, diction, metaphor, epic structure, and objections (chs. XIX–XXVI).

## Decision Rules

- Evaluate a narrative first by whether its events form a necessary or probable whole (chs. VII–IX).
- Prefer consequences arising from the plot itself over effects supplied only by staging (ch. XIV).
- Use reversal and recognition when they grow from the causal sequence, not from arbitrary surprise (chs. X–XI).
- Treat character as serving coherent action while still requiring consistency and appropriateness (ch. XV).

## Anti-patterns

- Episodic plots whose incidents have no necessary or probable sequence (ch. IX).
- Solving the central action by machinery rather than by what the plot establishes (ch. XV).
- Confusing historical particularity with poetry's concern for patterns of what could happen (ch. IX).
