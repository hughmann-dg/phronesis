# Politics — Book IV

## Core Idea

Political science must know not only the ideal constitution but also what is best for a given people, what is attainable from current conditions, and how regimes can be preserved.

## Reference Map

- Scope of constitutional expertise: ideal, circumstance-fit, attainable, and broadly suitable regimes (ch. 1).
- Varieties of democracy and oligarchy and the social parts underlying them (chs. 2–6).
- Aristocracy, polity, tyranny, and mixed constitutions (chs. 7–11).
- Matching institutions to social composition and balancing groups (chs. 12–13).
- Deliberative, executive, and judicial arrangements (chs. 14–16).

## Decision Rules

- Separate “best in principle” from “best feasible here” and “best transition from present conditions” (ch. 1).
- Design around the actual distribution of occupations, wealth, arms, and civic participation.
- Seek a substantial middle element where possible to reduce domination and faction (ch. 11).
- Treat constitutions as combinations of deliberative, executive, and judicial arrangements, not as single labels.
