# Reference Unit 7: Executable Limits

## Core Idea

Consequential analysis must expose uncertainty and distribution, while rights, consent, legality, dignity, and catastrophic-risk constraints remain independently reviewable.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Disaggregation Gate**: Show each stakeholder group before computing a total.
  - When to use: A score hides who bears harm.
  - How: Report benefit, harm, probability, magnitude, duration, reversibility, and confidence by group.
- **Rights Hard Stop**: Do not execute serious involuntary harm merely because a guessed aggregate is positive.
  - When to use: Privacy, bodily integrity, due process, discrimination, or basic security is implicated.
  - How: Pause for legal, rights, and domain review; search for constrained alternatives.
- **Uncertainty Sensitivity Test**: Vary empirical inputs and value weights that could reverse ranking.
  - When to use: Estimates are speculative.

## Key Concepts

- **Distribution**: Who receives benefits and harms.
- **Reversibility**: Ability to undo effects and compensate.
- **Moral uncertainty**: Uncertainty about values or principles, not only facts.
- **Constraint**: Requirement not traded away by ordinary aggregation.

## Anti-patterns

- **Spreadsheet laundering**: Turning guesses into authoritative decimals.
- **Stakeholder tokenism**: Listing groups but omitting their outcomes.
- **Compensation fantasy**: Assuming all harms can be repaid.

## Worked Example

A clinical system may improve average throughput but has uncertain error rates for an underrepresented population. Deployment stops at a monitored study with group-specific validation and human review rather than using average benefit to justify exposure.

## Key Takeaways

1. Show distribution before totals.
2. Preserve ranges and confidence.
3. Apply rights and legality independently.
4. Prefer reversible learning under uncertainty.
5. Label modern tools as interpretation.
