# Patterns — Mill Works

These are reconstructed decision patterns, not quotations.

## Stakeholder Consequence Map

**When to use**: Options distribute benefits and harms differently.

**How**: Identify affected groups; estimate direction, magnitude, probability, duration, reversibility, and confidence; show each group before aggregating.

**Trade-offs**: Makes impacts visible but risks false commensurability. (II, adapted)

## Quality-of-Welfare Test

**When to use**: Two benefits look equal only because the measure is crude.

**How**: Specify welfare dimensions; seek informed testimony from people with relevant comparative experience; record adaptation, coercion, and access biases.

**Trade-offs**: Adds qualitative judgment and possible elitism; require plural and affected perspectives. (II, adapted from competent judges)

## Motive-Act-Character Split

**When to use**: Good intent is being used to settle outcome quality, or a poor motive is being used to prove an act harmful.

**How**: Evaluate expected consequences, intention, and reliability of character on separate lines.

**Trade-offs**: Clarifies evaluation but does not erase legal or relational relevance of intent. (II)

## Justice Hard-Stop

**When to use**: Aggregate gains depend on concentrated involuntary harm.

**How**: Identify the protected claim, security interest, consent, alternatives, procedural fairness, and independent rights constraints before any aggregation.

**Trade-offs**: Prevents moral averaging; hard cases still require legal and rights expertise. (V, adapted)

## Sanction Map

**When to use**: A morally preferred policy may fail in implementation.

**How**: Map formal enforcement, social response, identity, conscience, and institutional reinforcement; test perverse incentives.

**Trade-offs**: Improves realism but can become manipulative if autonomy and transparency are ignored. (III, adapted)
