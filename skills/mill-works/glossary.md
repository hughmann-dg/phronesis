# Glossary — Mill's Utilitarianism

**Competent judges** — people acquainted with both compared modes of enjoyment, whose informed preference Mill uses to discuss quality (II).

**Expediency** — often used colloquially for narrow advantage; Mill distinguishes that usage from utility as the general interest (II, V).

**Greatest Happiness Principle** — standard connecting right action with promotion of happiness and prevention of the reverse (II).

**Higher and lower pleasures** — shorthand for qualitative distinctions Mill defends through informed preference (II).

**Impartiality** — equal consideration from a disinterested standpoint, not denial that special obligations can exist (II, V).

**Internal sanction** — inward feeling associated with conscience and moral obligation (III).

**Justice** — cluster involving duty, rights, punishment, impartiality, and security, analyzed in relation to utility (V).

**Motive** — why an agent acts; Mill distinguishes motive from the act's rightness (II).

**Right** — a valid claim on society's protection in Mill's analysis (V).

**Utility** — principle concerned with happiness and the interests of all affected, not merely convenience or organizational gain (II).

**Virtue** — capable, through association and cultivation, of becoming a desired part of happiness rather than only a means (IV).
