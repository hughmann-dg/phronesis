# Glossary

Definitions are interpretations of the listed edition passages, not quotations.

**Agency / what is up to us** - One's own present judgments, impulses, desires, aversions, and acts; narrower than everything one can influence. (Long, *Encheiridion* 1; Rolleston, I.2-5)

**Appearance / impression** - An initial presentation to thought that should be examined before assent. (Long, *Encheiridion* 1, 20, 34, 45; Rolleston, II.7)

**Assent** - Acceptance of an appearance as true or action-guiding; a main site of cognitive discipline. (Rolleston, II.4 and V.2; Long, *Encheiridion* 45)

**Attention** - Continuous watchfulness over the governing judgment in ordinary acts. (Long, selected Discourses, "On Attention")

**Aversion** - The orientation away from what is judged bad; training relocates it from unavoidable externals toward one's own wrongful action. (Long, *Encheiridion* 2; Rolleston, II.4)

**Character / role** - A form of conduct adopted or supplied by relationships and circumstances; it must be enacted within ability and ethical limits. (Long, *Encheiridion* 17, 33, 37; Rolleston, III.1)

**Desire** - The orientation toward what is judged good; outcome-dependent desire risks frustration when attached to what others control. (Long, *Encheiridion* 2 and 14)

**Discipline / ascesis** - Repeated exercises that weaken a harmful habit and strengthen a fitting response, performed without display. (Rolleston, V.2 and V.18)

**External** - A condition or outcome not wholly constituted by one's own act, including body, property, reputation, and office; not a synonym for worthless. (Long, *Encheiridion* 1; Rolleston, I.5)

**Freedom** - Non-subjection of one's judgment and action to demands for outcomes controlled by others. (Long, selected Discourses, "About Freedom"; *Encheiridion* 14 and 19)

**Governing faculty / master-faculty** - The capacity that evaluates appearances and directs the use of other abilities. (Rolleston, I.3-4)

**Indifferent** - Outside the strict location of moral good and evil in judgment and will; it does not entail equal preference, no causal importance, or no duty. (Rolleston, II.5-6; Long, "How Magnanimity Is Consistent with Care")

**Natural conception / preconception** - A broadly shared evaluative concept whose disputed application to particulars must be tested. (Rolleston, I.1-2; Long, "What the Beginning of Philosophy Is")

**Philosophy** - Training that changes desire, judgment, and conduct; explanation alone is insufficient. (Rolleston, I.1, I.5-7, V.17-25)

**Preferred outcome** - A modern interpretive label for an external condition worth pursuing without treating its occurrence as morally necessary or guaranteed. (Interpretation from Rolleston, II.5-6; Long, "How Magnanimity Is Consistent with Care")

**Progress** - Increasing consistency of desire, aversion, assent, and conduct rather than accumulation of explanations. (Long, "Of Progress or Improvement"; *Encheiridion* 48-51)

**Providence** - The ancient theological account of an ordered whole. In secular application, use only the limited operational lesson of accepting fixed facts while choosing one's response. (Rolleston, IV.1-4)

**Reserve clause / dual intention** - A modern name for pursuing an external aim while also intending to maintain sound judgment if conditions block it. (Long, *Encheiridion* 4)

**Tranquility** - Reduced disturbance produced by relocating demands from uncontrolled outcomes to responsible use of agency. (Long, "Of Tranquillity" and "About Freedom")

**Will** - Rolleston's term for the faculty of choice and use of appearances; translation choices vary across editions. (Rolleston, I.3-5)
