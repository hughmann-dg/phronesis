# Patterns

These are reconstructed inquiry patterns, not claims that Epictetus used modern framework names.

## Agency Map

**When to use**: Anxiety or planning treats a desired outcome as guaranteed by effort.

**How**:

1. State the event without evaluation.
2. List one's own judgments, commitments, communications, and next acts.
3. List factors one can influence but not determine.
4. List residual conditions controlled by others, chance, or completed facts.
5. Set a worthy action for the first list and a contingency for the others.

**Trade-offs**: A narrow map protects judgment but can hide collective power if "not mine alone" is mistaken for "cannot be changed." (Long, *Encheiridion* 1 and 14)

## Impression Audit

**When to use**: An insult, fear, temptation, or status signal creates immediate certainty.

**How**:

1. Pause before assent.
2. Record the observable presentation.
3. Name the interpretation added to it.
4. Test evidence and alternative interpretations.
5. Decide whether and how the impression should guide action.

**Trade-offs**: Reappraisal must not deny observable danger or shame people for emotion. (Long, *Encheiridion* 5, 20, 34, 45)

## Dual-Intention Plan

**When to use**: A plan matters, but execution depends on other people or uncertain conditions.

**How**:

1. Define the external objective.
2. Forecast ordinary friction.
3. Define the conduct standard to preserve through that friction.
4. Choose a next act and fallback consistent with both intentions.
5. Judge the outcome and the decision process separately.

**Trade-offs**: Inner consistency does not excuse a poor plan or harmful outcome. (Long, *Encheiridion* 4 and 10)

## Desire-to-Preference Rewrite

**When to use**: Language such as "must," "cannot bear," or "need them to" turns preference into dependence.

**How**:

1. Identify the demanded outcome.
2. Rewrite it as a strong but conditional preference.
3. Name why the outcome still matters.
4. Place non-negotiability on one's ethical constraint or duty instead.
5. Prepare for the residual outcome without rehearsing helplessness.

**Trade-offs**: Do not weaken genuine rights, safety requirements, or obligations into mere preferences. (Long, *Encheiridion* 2 and 14)

## Preconception Dispute Test

**When to use**: Parties agree on values in the abstract but reach incompatible judgments.

**How**:

1. Name the shared evaluative term.
2. Ask how each party applies it to the particulars.
3. Identify the contradiction.
4. Demand a criterion beyond "it seems so to me."
5. Test the criterion consistently across comparable cases.

**Trade-offs**: Epictetus supplies the structure of inquiry, not a complete modern moral theory. (Rolleston, I.1-2)

## Commitment Price Test

**When to use**: A person is attracted to a role, project, identity, or status but has not examined its costs.

**How**:

1. Specify the role or outcome sought.
2. List prerequisites, recurring disciplines, opportunity costs, and likely reversals.
3. Test fit with ability, responsibilities, and ethical constraints.
4. Accept the full price, redesign the commitment, or decline it.
5. Set evidence of follow-through.

**Trade-offs**: Ability is developable; the test should prevent fantasy, not enforce a fixed social place. (Rolleston, II.27; Long, *Encheiridion* 29 and 37)

## Role-and-Relationship Check

**When to use**: A conflict tempts retaliation, withdrawal, or blind obedience.

**How**:

1. Name the actual relationship and any formal duty.
2. Identify the conduct the relationship reasonably asks of you.
3. Apply independent constraints: safety, rights, truthfulness, and competence.
4. Distinguish your duty from the other party's response.
5. Act without using the role to excuse abuse or domination.

**Trade-offs**: Ancient role ethics contains hierarchy that must not be preserved. (Rolleston, III.1; Long, *Encheiridion* 24, 30, 32, 43)

## Habit Counterpractice

**When to use**: The same anger, avoidance, distraction, or indulgence recurs.

**How**:

1. Identify the triggering appearance.
2. Interrupt the practiced sequence early.
3. Substitute a concrete response incompatible with the unwanted habit.
4. Track repetitions and repair lapses without rationalization.
5. Look for changed conduct, not self-description.

**Trade-offs**: Severe compulsions, trauma, or illness may require clinical help rather than self-training alone. (Rolleston, V.2, V.17-19)

## Outcome-Luck Separation

**When to use**: A good outcome is used to justify a reckless decision, or a bad outcome to condemn a sound one.

**How**:

1. Reconstruct what was knowable at decision time.
2. Evaluate evidence, intention, and chosen action.
3. Evaluate preparation for downside.
4. Evaluate the realized outcome separately.
5. Learn from both without allowing luck to rewrite the process.

**Trade-offs**: Decision quality and outcome quality are distinct but both matter, especially when others bear risk. (Interpretation from Long, *Encheiridion* 4, 9-10; Rolleston, II.5-6)
