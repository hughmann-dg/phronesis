## Core Frameworks and Mental Models

### Locate agency narrowly

Separate one's own present judgments, impulses, desires, aversions, and acts from body, property, reputation, office, other people, and completed outcomes. The point is not that externals cannot be influenced or matter morally; it is that they are not wholly "up to us." Commit to a worthy act without making inner freedom depend on a guaranteed result. (Long, *Encheiridion* 1; Rolleston, Book I, chs. 2-5)

### Test the impression before assent

An appearance is not yet a verdict. Pause, describe the observable event, separate it from the value judgment added to it, test what lies within agency, and assent only as far as the evidence warrants. This is a discipline of judgment, not denial of facts or feelings. (Long, *Encheiridion* 1, 5, 20, 34, 45; Rolleston, Book II, chs. 4 and 7)

### Use a dual intention

For any undertaking, hold both an external aim and an agency aim: accomplish the task if conditions permit, and preserve sound judgment and conduct whatever occurs. Anticipate ordinary friction without demanding that reality conform to the plan. (Long, *Encheiridion* 4 and 10)

### Train desire and aversion

Attaching necessity to an outcome controlled by others creates dependency and fear. Convert demands into conditional preferences, place aversion chiefly on one's own wrongful action, and keep impulses measured while learning. This is not a ban on goals, planning, or protecting people from harm. (Long, *Encheiridion* 2 and 14; Rolleston, Book II, ch. 4)

### Apply shared concepts to particulars

People can agree on abstract words such as good, just, and useful while applying them incompatibly. Begin inquiry by distrusting bare seeming, exposing the disputed application, and seeking a defensible criterion. (Rolleston, Book I, chs. 1-2)

### Train three connected fields

Coordinate pursuit and avoidance, fitting conduct within relationships, and careful assent to appearances. Technical argument cannot substitute for trained desire or responsible action. (Rolleston, Book II, ch. 4; Long, *Encheiridion* 51)

### Price a commitment before choosing it

Look at prerequisites, costs, likely setbacks, and follow-through before adopting a role or project. Accept the price knowingly or decline the commitment; repeated enthusiasm without paying its costs fragments character and execution. (Rolleston, Book II, ch. 27; Long, *Encheiridion* 29 and 37)

### Derive duties from relationships

Ask what good conduct is required by one's actual relationship—as colleague, neighbor, citizen, caregiver, friend, or office-holder—while retaining fidelity and judgment. A role supplies a question, not automatic obedience or permission for abuse. (Rolleston, Book III, ch. 1; Long, *Encheiridion* 24, 30, 32, 43)

### Practice by repetition and substitution

Actions strengthen corresponding habits. Interrupt a harmful pattern early, rehearse the alternative response, track repetitions, and seek evidence in conduct. Philosophy is incomplete until its precepts are used. (Rolleston, Book V, chs. 2, 17-19, 24-25; Long, *Encheiridion* 46-51)

### Preserve care without outcome dependence

External outcomes can be preferred, planned for, and pursued carefully even though they are not wholly within agency. Confidence concerns one's governing judgment; caution concerns how one uses circumstances. (Rolleston, Book II, chs. 5-6; Long, selected Discourses, "How Magnanimity Is Consistent with Care")

## Sources and Limits

The immutable local texts and records live under `sources/corpus/` and are ignored by Git. Tracked provenance is in `sources/manifest.yaml`. Rights verification is scoped to the United States; check local law elsewhere.

Epictetus wrote no surviving work himself; Arrian recorded the teachings, and all three editions here are selections or later arrangements. The skill therefore distinguishes section-level evidence from editor arrangement and disputed attribution.

This skill synthesizes rather than quotes and never authorizes personality role-play. Ancient claims about slavery, women, social rank, disability, providence, suicide, and emotional invulnerability are not imported as executable norms. "Outside agency" never means unimportant, immune from influence, or undeserving of prevention. For self-harm risk, abuse, acute grief, medical danger, or structural injustice, use appropriate safety, professional, and institutional resources rather than this lens alone.
