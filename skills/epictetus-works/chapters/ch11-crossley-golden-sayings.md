# Crossley Golden Sayings - Arrangement and Corroboration

## Core Idea

Crossley's 189 numbered sayings provide a compact arranged traversal of recurring themes, useful for comparison and retrieval but weaker than work-based locators for establishing doctrine.

## Reference Map

- Rational purpose and civic participation (I-II, XV-XVI).
- Worth, character, and what belongs to judgment (VIII-XII, XX-XXIV).
- Freedom, dependency, and governance of judgment (XXIX-XXXIV, XLI).
- Externals, blame, fear, and death (XLIX-LVI).
- Training, habit, and the price of tranquility (LXXIII-LXXV, CIV-CVI).
- Reasoning, exercise, and progress shown in action (CXVII-CXVIII, CXXVI, CXXXV).
- Assigned parts, conduct in company, and social relations (CLX-CLXXIII).
- Adversity reports, solitude, mortality, and relational duties (CLXXXVII-CLXXXIX).

## Decision Rules

- Use a saying as a retrieval lead, then locate its parallel in Long or Rolleston before promoting it into executable doctrine.
- Treat Crossley's numbering as an arranger's sequence, not the original order of Arrian's Discourses.
- When the same theme appears across editions, preserve translation differences rather than fusing wording into a false quotation.
- Prefer Crossley for theme comparison and compact examples; prefer Long's Encheiridion sections or named Discourses headings for precise public citations.
- Distinguish Golden Sayings I-CLXXXIX from Appendix A's uncertain fragments and Appendix B's Cleanthes hymn.

## Worked Example

Crossley CIV describes the price of athletic or philosophical commitment, while Long's Encheiridion 29 and Rolleston II.27 provide extended parallel treatments. A skill may reconstruct a Commitment Price Test from the shared structure: identify prerequisites, recurring discipline, possible defeat, opportunity costs, and fit. It should cite Long 29 or Rolleston II.27 for the main doctrine and Crossley CIV as comparison, not quote a blended English sentence.

## Translation Comparison

Crossley's arrangement often favors short, memorable units. That compactness can obscure surrounding qualifications. For example, freedom passages clustered around XXIX-XXXIV can sound like complete independence, while Long and Rolleston more clearly connect freedom to the use of appearances, desire, and relationships. Likewise, the late mortality passages CLXXXVII-CLXXXIX sit within an arranged ethical arc and should not be isolated as crisis advice.

## Limits

Memorability increases the risk of sloganizing. A terse saying may be rhetorically forceful while under-specifying material harm, social power, or the needs of a grieving person. Crossley's older English also reflects editorial and cultural choices distinct from the ancient source.
