# Transmission and Edition Boundaries

## Core Idea

The surviving material is mediated: Epictetus taught orally, Arrian recorded and compiled teachings, and the three verified English editions used here select and arrange that inheritance differently. Sound use begins by citing the specific edition and internal locator rather than an undifferentiated "Epictetus says."

## Reference Map

- Epictetus's oral teaching and Arrian's role as recorder/compiler (Rolleston, Introduction).
- Long's biographical note, selected Discourses, and complete 52-section Encheiridion (Long, Biographical Note and contents).
- Rolleston's five-book thematic arrangement of Encheiridion, Dissertations, and Fragments (Rolleston, title page, Introduction, Books I-V).
- Crossley's 189 numbered Golden Sayings, followed by "Fragments Attributed to Epictetus" and a separate Cleanthes appendix (Crossley, contents and appendices).

## Source Controls

- Use `epictetus-discourses-enchiridion` when a Long heading or Encheiridion section supplies the claim.
- Use `epictetus-teaching-rolleston` when a Book/chapter locator supplies the claim. Rolleston's Books I-V are an editorial arrangement, not the original book order of Arrian's Discourses.
- Use `epictetus-golden-sayings-crossley` primarily for comparison and compact retrieval. Crossley describes the text as translated and arranged, and labels the later fragments as attributed.
- Do not infer the wording of the Greek from agreement among old English translations.
- Do not quote this skill. Retrieve the local source text and verify the surrounding section.

## Decision Rules

- When a doctrine depends on wording such as "in our power," "will," or "appearance," compare at least Long and Rolleston before fixing an English formulation.
- When Crossley supplies a vivid maxim, find its parallel in Long or Rolleston before treating it as a central doctrine.
- When a claim appears only in the attributed-fragments appendix, label the attribution uncertainty.
- When the Cleanthes hymn is relevant to Stoic providence, cite Cleanthes, not Epictetus.
- Prefer work-section locators over paragraph numbers in authored synthesis; paragraph locators remain useful for corpus retrieval.

## Worked Example

A decision adviser wants to state that Epictetus divides the world into "what I control" and "what I do not control." Long's Encheiridion 1 lists opinion, impulse, desire, aversion, and one's own acts as in one's power, while body, property, reputation, and office are not. Rolleston I.2-5 uses "our own power" and centers will and the use of appearances. The responsible synthesis is therefore narrower than ordinary managerial control: identify what is constituted by one's own judgment and action, then distinguish influenceable conditions and residual outcomes as a modern operational addition. It would be inaccurate to claim that Epictetus supplied today's common three-circle control/influence/concern diagram.

## Limits

The editions reflect nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century vocabulary and editorial choices. They include ancient theological premises, examples involving slavery, hierarchical social roles, gender prescriptions, and stark claims about bereavement and death. These are primary-source facts to interpret, not executable defaults.

## Source Basis

- `epictetus-teaching-rolleston`: Introduction; editorial structure of Books I-V.
- `epictetus-discourses-enchiridion`: Biographical Note; selected Discourses; Encheiridion 1-52.
- `epictetus-golden-sayings-crossley`: Golden Sayings I-CLXXXIX; Appendix A; Cleanthes appendix.
