# Rolleston Book III - Relations, Friendship, and Public Action

## Core Idea

Agency is exercised socially: identify obligations from actual relationships, preserve one's own fidelity, and refuse to make cooperation depend on controlling another person's judgment.

## Reference Map

- Obligations derived from relationships such as family, neighbor, citizen, and office (ch. 1).
- Arguments for human sociality and against reducing good to bodily pleasure (chs. 2-3).
- Slavery as a metaphor for dependency on externals, alongside historically situated literal slavery (ch. 4).
- Public administration and the question of what counts as genuine civic benefit (chs. 5-6).
- Friendship and conflict under competing conceptions of advantage (ch. 7).
- Change, loss, solitude, and social presence (chs. 8-9).
- Contentiousness, revenge, and the human qualities expressed in conduct (ch. 10).

## Decision Rules

- Start a duty analysis by naming the relationship and office actually held (ch. 1).
- Evaluate what maintaining one's own role requires without making good conduct conditional on the other party behaving well (ch. 1).
- Add independent moral constraints: a relationship never makes abuse, deception, domination, or unsafe compliance obligatory.
- In public action, distinguish symbolic provision and status from the formation of trustworthy citizens and institutions (chs. 5-6).
- When friendship or family conflict escalates, identify which external each party treats as the decisive good; rivalry often follows the perceived scarcity of status, property, or control (ch. 7).
- Treat solitude as a question of one's capacity for reflective activity, not simply physical isolation (ch. 9).
- Answer contentious or vengeful conduct with boundaries and reasoned action, not a mirrored loss of one's own standards (ch. 10).

## Interpretation Notes

Book III contains both a strong social thesis and ancient hierarchy. Executable use keeps the relational insight while rejecting slavery, fixed rank, patriarchal authority, and unconditional submission. "Hold your own place" means preserve responsible conduct under modern rights and role constraints, not remain in danger or accept injustice.

The claim that conflict follows mistaken views of the good can illuminate incentive disputes, but it must not reduce deliberate exploitation to mere misunderstanding. Evidence about power and incentives remains necessary.

## Worked Example

A manager receives a hostile message from a colleague. The role check identifies duties to the project, team safety, truthfulness, and fair process. The manager does not control whether the colleague retracts the message, but does control documentation, a calm reply, escalation through policy, and protection of affected staff. Book III does not require passivity or private endurance. It asks the manager to avoid retaliatory humiliation while performing the office responsibly and using institutional remedies.

## Failure Modes

- Turning role ethics into automatic obedience.
- Treating systemic exploitation as a private judgment problem.
- Using social harmony to silence necessary conflict.
- Assuming another's wrongdoing eliminates one's own duty to set boundaries or seek remedy.
