# Rolleston Book I - Philosophy, Preconceptions, and Agency

## Core Idea

Philosophy begins by distrusting unsupported seeming, testing how shared evaluative concepts are applied to disputed particulars, and training the governing faculty that uses appearances.

## Reference Map

- Awareness of error and the need for a criterion beyond seeming (chs. 1-2).
- Reason as the faculty that evaluates its own use and directs other capacities (ch. 3).
- Moral good located in the disposition and use of will, with externals as material for action (ch. 4).
- The agency distinction and examination of harsh appearances (ch. 5).
- Philosophy as a learned practice expressed in desire, conduct, and relationships (chs. 6-7).
- The Cynic as an extreme vocation with demanding prerequisites, not a costume or persona (ch. 8).

## Decision Rules

- If parties agree that justice, usefulness, or integrity matters but disagree about a case, inspect how each maps the shared concept to the particulars (chs. 1-2).
- Do not accept "it seems right to me" as a sufficient criterion when incompatible judgments are in play (ch. 1).
- Ask which faculty should decide whether and when a specialist ability is used; technical competence does not choose its own end (ch. 3).
- Evaluate external resources through the quality of their use rather than treating possession itself as proof of worth (ch. 4).
- Treat an urgent appearance as a claim to examine, especially when it concerns reputation, property, office, or another person's action (ch. 5).
- Look for learning in changed conduct and relationships, not in verbal fluency alone (chs. 6-7).

## Interpretation Notes

Rolleston's "Will" and "master-faculty" translate an ancient account of rational agency. Executable counsel should not inflate this into omnipotent self-control. Health, coercion, disability, material deprivation, and institutions constrain action even when they do not by themselves settle the moral quality of a person's choice.

The claim that externals are not the location of moral good does not make them causally or ethically negligible. Book I itself presents external conditions as material to be used, and chapter 6 links practice to conduct as family member, neighbor, citizen, ruler, or subject.

## Worked Example

Two teams both say a migration must be "responsible." One means meeting the announced date; the other means avoiding a poorly tested cutover. Following chs. 1-2, do not debate responsibility in the abstract. List the particulars each application treats as decisive: support expiry, rollback duration, incident exposure, commitments, and affected people. Ask for a criterion that would apply consistently to a comparable migration. Then use ch. 3 to keep technical expertise subordinate to the decision's governing objective, and ch. 5 to examine whether fear of embarrassment is masquerading as evidence.

## Failure Modes

- Treating one's preferred application of a value as self-evident.
- Using the agency distinction to ignore material constraints.
- Mistaking rhetorical or logical skill for ethical progress.
- Reproducing the Cynic vocation as severe lifestyle theater or historical role-play.
