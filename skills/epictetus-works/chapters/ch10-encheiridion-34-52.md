# Encheiridion 34-52 - Delay, Discipline, and Embodied Precepts

## Core Idea

The final sections convert doctrine into micro-practices: delay before assent, preserve social value in ordinary choices, distinguish description from moral judgment, train privately, and make conduct—not exposition—the test of learning.

## Reference Map

- Delay and temporal comparison before pleasure; act openly once judgment is settled (34-35).
- Social value, role fit, governing faculty, and limits on consumption (36-41).
- Interpreting others, choosing a bearable relational frame, and avoiding invalid status inferences (42-44).
- Description before judgment and conduct before philosophical display (45-47).
- Signs of progress, interpretation versus use, and ending procrastination (48-50).
- Precept, proof, and meta-proof ordered toward practice; maxims held ready (51-52).

## Decision Rules

- Delay an attractive impulse long enough to compare immediate and later consequences, then decide rather than merely suppress (34).
- Once an action is judged right on adequate grounds, do not let mistaken audience opinion alone reverse it (35).
- Include relational and social value alongside private consumption value (36).
- Decline a role beyond current ability or redesign it, while remembering that ability can be developed (37).
- Protect the governing judgment with the same anticipatory care used to avoid bodily injury (38).
- State observable behavior before adding moral evaluation (45).
- Let actions demonstrate principles; avoid extracting status from austerity or philosophical vocabulary (46-49).
- Stop waiting for a perfect teacher or future date when the next practice is already known (50).
- Use proof to support a precept, but return to the conduct the precept governs (51).

## Worked Example

An executive receives a flattering acquisition offer. Section 34 inserts a pause and asks about both anticipated gratification and likely later regret. Section 45 rewrites "they finally recognize our worth" as observable terms: price, control provisions, retention conditions, and public claims. Section 36 adds effects on employees and partners. Section 37 tests whether the executive can perform the post-acquisition role. The eventual answer may still be yes; the exercise prevents charm and identity from substituting for due diligence.

## Critical Limits

Section 40 contains an ancient prescription about women's value and modesty that is not executable guidance. Reject its gender hierarchy. Section 41's suspicion of bodily concern must not discourage health care, disability accommodation, nutrition, rest, or physical training. Section 42 can support interpretive charity but does not require tolerating abuse or abandoning accountability.

The text's inward standard of progress can become self-surveillance or shame. Modern practice should use proportionate tracking, repair, and outside support rather than perfectionism.
