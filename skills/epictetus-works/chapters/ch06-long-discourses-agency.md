# Long Selected Discourses - Agency, Progress, and Particulars

## Core Idea

Long's selected Discourses expand the Encheiridion's compact rules into a practice of governing appearances, diagnosing false measures of progress, and applying shared preconceptions to concrete cases.

## Reference Map

- Rational faculty and the distinction between what is and is not one's own ("Of the Things Which Are in Our Power and Not in Our Power").
- Character preserved through rational use rather than external success ("How a Man on Every Occasion Can Maintain His Proper Character").
- Progress located in desire, aversion, pursuit, and assent rather than explanation ("Of Progress or Improvement").
- Philosophy's promise concerns one's own governing part, not control of another ("What Philosophy Promises").
- Shared preconceptions and their disputed application ("What the Beginning of Philosophy Is"; "How We Must Adapt Preconceptions to Particular Cases").
- Active resistance to being carried away by appearances ("How We Should Struggle Against Appearances").
- Practice directed to one's weakest domain of desire or aversion ("We Ought Chiefly to Practise Ourselves").

## Decision Rules

- Ask first what faculty is competent to decide whether a specialized ability should be used; technique alone cannot supply purpose.
- Keep the locus of agency in the use of appearances and one's own act, not in the body or reputation being immune to interference.
- Reject output proxies such as books read, terminology mastered, or arguments won when conduct has not changed.
- When asking how another person can be made to stop, restate the problem as what judgment, boundary, request, or action is available to oneself.
- Surface the shared preconception—good, fair, useful, courageous—then test how it is applied to the disputed facts.
- Train where impressions repeatedly defeat judgment rather than where practice is easy or impressive.

## Worked Example

A leader asks how to make a resistant department support a change. "What Philosophy Promises" blocks the fantasy that counsel can govern the other department's will. The agency map identifies the leader's explanation, negotiation, incentives, evidence, escalation path, and fallback. "What the Beginning of Philosophy Is" then asks what both groups call a successful change and where they apply that idea differently. "Of Progress" prevents the leader from counting the number of presentations as progress if trust, clarity, and commitments remain unchanged.

## Interpretation Notes

Long's language of freedom and injury is morally internal: coercion can damage bodies, resources, opportunities, and communities even if it does not force a person to endorse the coercer's values. Modern counsel must name those harms plainly and support prevention and remedy.

The text's treatment of error as a mistaken appearance can support patience, but it does not establish that every harmful actor is merely confused. Strategic and institutional analysis may be required.
