# Encheiridion 1-14 - Agency, Desire, and Judgment

## Core Idea

The opening sequence relocates unconditional demands from outcomes to one's own judgment and action, then supplies compact exercises for testing appearances and preserving a dual intention under friction.

## Reference Map

- What is and is not in one's power; harsh appearances tested by that rule (1).
- Desire and aversion disciplined during training (2).
- Attachment described through the changeable nature of its object (3).
- The bath exercise: anticipate friction and intend both the act and sound conduct (4).
- Disturbance mediated by opinions (5-6).
- Temporary use of externals, acceptance of events, and impediments distinguished from will (7-9).
- Finding a fitting capacity in each event (10).
- Loss framed as return; small costs accepted for tranquility (11-12).
- Status humility and freedom from demands on other people (13-14).

## Decision Rules

- Put judgments, impulses, desires, aversions, and one's own acts in the agency column; place body, property, reputation, office, and other people's actions outside complete agency (1).
- When a harsh appearance arrives, label it as an appearance and test it before treating it as reality (1).
- During early training, reduce outcome-dependent desire rather than multiplying goals that make freedom contingent on others (2).
- Before an undertaking, forecast ordinary interference and add a second intention to preserve fitting conduct (4).
- Separate the event from the evaluative proposition producing disturbance, while still investigating the event (5).
- Ask which available capacity—patience, endurance, restraint, inquiry, or courage—fits the circumstance (10).
- Start habit change on small irritants where the cost of a new response is manageable (12).

## Worked Example

Before a contentious meeting, the external aim is approval of a proposal. The agency aim is to present evidence honestly, listen, avoid retaliation, and record next steps. Likely friction includes interruption, delay, and criticism. If approval fails, the plan is revised; the meeting is not declared a success merely because the speaker stayed calm. Section 4 separates the process standard from the result while preserving both as objects of evaluation.

## Critical Limits

Section 3 and section 11 use bereavement to teach impermanence in language that can be harsh and dehumanizing. Do not deploy those formulations to someone in acute grief. Validate the loss, provide care, and use this lens only if the person wants it.

Section 5 does not establish that events never cause distress or harm; trauma, illness, coercion, and deprivation have real psychological and material effects. The actionable claim is narrower: interpretations can amplify or redirect distress and should be examined when possible.

The agency distinction is binary at the doctrinal level but modern planning benefits from three operational columns: one's own act, influence without determination, and residual outcome. Do not attribute that three-part diagram to Epictetus.
