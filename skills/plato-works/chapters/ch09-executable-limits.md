# Reference Unit 9: Executable Limits

## Core Idea

Socratic examination helps expose unstable terms and unsupported premises, but it must end in decision-relevant evidence or action and must yield to urgent safety, specialist expertise, and independent rights constraints.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Question Budget**: Prioritize questions by expected decision value.
  - When to use: Examination could expand indefinitely.
  - How: Rank unknowns by option-changing potential, tractability, and delay cost.
- **Expertise Respect Gate**: Challenge scope and evidence without treating verified expertise as mere status.
  - When to use: Specialized technical, medical, legal, or safety claims matter.
  - How: Check credentials, domain fit, evidence quality, and independent corroboration.
- **Urgency Stop**: Act on immediate safety needs before completing philosophical clarification.
  - When to use: Delay materially increases harm.
  - How: Take the safest authorized step, preserve facts, and resume examination afterward.

## Key Concepts

- **Decision value**: Likelihood an answer changes the choice multiplied by importance.
- **Deferral**: Routing to a more suitable method or expert.
- **Operational definition**: Decision-sufficient meaning adopted without claiming metaphysical finality.
- **Synthesis**: Moving from questions to a bounded conclusion or experiment.

## Mental Models

- Examination is a phase in a decision process, not the whole process.
- Calibrated humility permits action under uncertainty.

## Anti-patterns

- **Endless elenchus**: Questioning without decision ownership.
- **Contrarian performance**: Attacking every premise equally.
- **Emergency seminar**: Delaying protection to perfect the framing.
- **Historical authority**: Letting ancient prestige substitute for evidence.

## Worked Example

A security incident has uncertain cause but active exposure. The examiner does not debate the definition of breach before containment. The team isolates affected systems, preserves evidence, then examines causal assumptions and notification obligations with specialists.

## Key Takeaways

1. Rank questions by decision value.
2. Stop at a usable operational definition.
3. Respect verified expertise.
4. Protect safety before extended inquiry.
5. End with synthesis or a next test.
