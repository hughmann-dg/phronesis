# Reference Unit 7: Gorgias

## Core Idea

*Gorgias* examines rhetoric's object and power, distinguishing persuasion from knowledge and craft from gratification, then broadens into disputes about power, injustice, punishment, the good life, and political conduct.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Persuasion-Knowledge Split**: Determine whether discourse produces belief or teachable understanding. (*Gorgias* 454c-461b)
  - When to use: A presentation changes confidence quickly.
  - How: Ask what mechanism, evidence, or competence the audience can now demonstrate.
- **Craft-Flattery Test**: Ask whether a practice has knowledge of its end and standards of correction or merely optimizes approval. (462b-466a)
  - When to use: A metric rewards immediate engagement.
  - How: State the claimed good, causal model, feedback, and failure criterion.
- **Apparent-Power Test**: Distinguish doing what seems best from achieving what one genuinely wants under a sound account of the good. (466a-481b)
  - When to use: Formal authority is equated with effective agency.
  - How: Trace action through consequences, dependencies, and value assumptions.

## Key Concepts

- **Belief-producing persuasion**: Conviction without the knowledge at issue (454e-455a).
- **Rhetoric**: Practice whose nature and value are contested throughout the dialogue.
- **Flattery**: Class of gratification-producing practices in Socrates' analogy (463a-466a).
- **Power**: Disputed capacity to do what one wills or what seems best (466a-470a).

## Mental Models

- Measure teaching by transferable competence, not audience confidence.
- Treat reach, authority, and attention as means whose value depends on ends and effects.

## Anti-patterns

- **Rhetoric dismissal**: Assuming all persuasion is corrupt.
- **Engagement-as-good**: Optimizing response without a defensible objective.
- **Authority-as-agency**: Ignoring dependencies and consequences.

## Worked Example

A vendor demo makes executives confident but leaves technical reviewers unable to reproduce the benchmark. The split classifies the event as persuasion, requests test conditions and independent replication, and delays the purchase without denying the possible product value.

## Key Takeaways

1. Separate belief from knowledge.
2. Ask what end a practice can explain and correct toward.
3. Evaluate apparent power through actual consequences.
4. Keep ethical conclusions separate from rhetorical effectiveness.
