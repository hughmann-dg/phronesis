# Reference Unit 5: Crito

## Core Idea

*Crito* examines whether escaping an unjust sentence is consistent with principles against wrongdoing and with obligations attributed to law and city; it is a conflict of duties, not a simple command to obey.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Reputation-versus-Reason Test**: Do not let the crowd's opinion settle what is right. (*Crito* 44c-48a)
  - When to use: Fear of public reaction dominates the decision.
  - How: Identify relevant expertise, actual consequences, and principled reasons.
- **Non-Retaliation Constraint**: Test whether suffered wrong is being used to license another wrong. (49a-e)
  - When to use: Revenge or reciprocity drives action.
  - How: Evaluate the proposed act independently from the provocation.
- **Agreement and Institutional Obligation Audit**: Examine benefits received, ability to contest or leave, and consequences for legal order. (50a-54e)
  - When to use: Membership is said to imply consent.
  - How: Test whether agreement was free, informed, revisable, and compatible with rights.

## Key Concepts

- **Wrongdoing**: Constraint not erased by another's injustice.
- **Agreement**: Basis invoked by the personified Laws.
- **Laws speech**: Dramatic argument that must not be treated as unqualified authorial doctrine.
- **Consistency**: Whether present action contradicts principles previously accepted.

## Mental Models

- Separate retaliation from remedy.
- Treat institutional loyalty as defeasible when consent, justice, or safety fails.

## Anti-patterns

- **Obedience absolutism**: Using *Crito* to demand compliance with injustice.
- **Victim constraint only**: Scrutinizing the harmed party while ignoring institutional wrongdoing.
- **Reputation panic**: Acting primarily to avoid what others may say.

## Worked Example

An employee considers disclosing misconduct despite a confidentiality term. The audit distinguishes retaliation from protective reporting, tests voluntariness and scope of the agreement, assesses legal channels and risks, and seeks specialist advice rather than declaring all promises absolute.

## Key Takeaways

1. Evaluate the act apart from provocation.
2. Test consent and agreement conditions.
3. Separate remedy from revenge.
4. Apply modern rights and legal review.
