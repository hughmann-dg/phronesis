# Reference Unit 1: Edition and Attribution Boundaries

## Core Idea

The source is Jowett's 1892 third-edition Volume II: six principal dialogues plus appendix dialogues, introductions, analyses, and marginal apparatus. Every claim must identify which layer supplies it.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Layer Check**: Separate dialogue, speaker, translator wording, introduction, marginal analysis, and project interpretation.
  - When to use: A polished summary is about to be attributed to Plato.
  - How: Locate the passage after the dialogue heading; identify speaker and Stephanus guide; label synthesis.
- **Locator Verification**: Treat Gutenberg page guides as routing aids, then verify letter subdivisions in the rendered edition.
  - When to use: Quoting or making a fine-grained doctrinal claim.
  - How: Retrieve `plato-dialogues`, open the rendered page, and confirm dialogue/page/letter.

## Key Concepts

- **Third edition**: Jowett's revised and corrected five-volume edition (1892).
- **Paratext**: Jowett's introductions, analyses, headings, and notes surrounding the dialogues.
- **Dramatic speaker**: Character whose statement is not automatically Plato's own conclusion.
- **Stephanus reference**: Conventional dialogue page-and-letter locator.

## Mental Models

- Treat a dialogue as an argument staged among speakers, not a list of authorial propositions.
- Treat translation choice as evidence that must remain visible in close interpretation.

## Anti-patterns

- **Speaker collapse**: Attributing every Socratic or interlocutor claim directly to Plato.
- **Paratext laundering**: Presenting Jowett's analysis as ancient text.
- **Locator confidence**: Inventing a letter subdivision not preserved in the machine text.

## Worked Example

A claim that Plato defines rhetoric as mere persuasion is routed to *Gorgias* 454c-461b. The reviewer confirms that the distinction occurs in the dialogue rather than Jowett's introduction, names the speaker context, and paraphrases unless the rendered text has been checked.

## Key Takeaways

1. Retrieve the primary edition before quoting.
2. Identify speaker and textual layer.
3. Verify fine locators visually.
4. Label executable patterns as interpretation.
