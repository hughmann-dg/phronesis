# Reference Unit 6: Phaedo

## Core Idea

*Phaedo* combines a narrated death scene with arguments concerning philosophy, soul, recollection, affinity, forms, causation, hypothesis, and objections; its reasoning is important but not a substitute for empirical evidence.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Argument Sequence Map**: Track premises, objections, repairs, and distinct arguments rather than merging them. (*Phaedo* 69e-107b)
  - When to use: Several supports for one conclusion are treated as a single proof.
  - How: List each argument, conclusion, objection, and remaining dependency.
- **Misology Warning**: Failure of arguments can provoke indiscriminate distrust of reasoning. (89d-91c)
  - When to use: A bad forecast leads to rejection of all analysis.
  - How: Diagnose the failed model and improve standards rather than abandoning reason.
- **Hypothesis Method**: Work from a hypothesis and test consequences while reserving examination of the hypothesis itself. (99d-107b)
  - When to use: Action requires a provisional model.
  - How: State the assumption, implications, falsifiers, and revision trigger.

## Key Concepts

- **Recollection**: Argument linking learning with prior cognition (72e-77a).
- **Affinity**: Comparison between visible/composite and invisible/intelligible (78b-84b).
- **Forms**: Explanatory entities invoked in the later causal discussion (100b-105c).
- **Misology**: Hatred or distrust of argument after repeated disappointment (89d-91c).

## Mental Models

- Treat multiple weak arguments as multiple, not automatically cumulative.
- Use hypotheses provisionally and expose their revision conditions.

## Anti-patterns

- **Metaphysics-as-data**: Treating the soul arguments as modern empirical findings.
- **Argument bundling**: Counting distinct arguments without testing shared assumptions.
- **Misology**: Rejecting reasoning because some reasoners failed.

## Worked Example

A team has three forecasts supporting expansion, all derived from the same retention assumption. The sequence map reveals that apparent triangulation is one shared dependency. The team treats retention as a hypothesis and sets a pilot threshold.

## Key Takeaways

1. Map arguments separately.
2. Identify shared premises.
3. State hypotheses and revision triggers.
4. Do not convert metaphysics into empirical counsel.
