# Reference Unit 2: Meno

## Core Idea

*Meno* moves from an apparently practical question—whether virtue can be taught—to disputes about definition, the paralysis of inquiry, recollection, and the distinction between true opinion and knowledge.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **One-Account Test**: Ask what a thing is before cataloguing examples. (*Meno* 71a-79e)
  - When to use: Participants agree on a word but not its decision meaning.
  - How: Request one account, test its scope, and distinguish property, instance, and definition.
- **Aporia as Diagnostic**: Recognition of not knowing can improve inquiry. (79e-80d)
  - When to use: Confident assumptions fail counterexample tests.
  - How: Record the failed answers and turn the central unknown into research or experiment.
- **Opinion-Knowledge Distinction**: A true belief can guide action but lacks the stability associated with an explanatory account. (97a-98a)
  - When to use: A correct forecast may have been lucky or poorly grounded.
  - How: Ask what connects the conclusion to repeatable evidence or explanation.

## Key Concepts

- **Virtue**: The dialogue's disputed object of inquiry.
- **Meno's paradox**: Challenge about how inquiry can seek what it does not know (80d-e).
- **Recollection**: Response developed through the geometrical examination (81a-86c).
- **True opinion**: Correct belief distinguished from knowledge (97a-98a).

## Mental Models

- Use failed definitions to map the real disagreement.
- Treat correct outcome and sound reasoning as separate evaluation axes.

## Anti-patterns

- **Example pile**: Listing instances instead of defining the category.
- **Aporia theatre**: Celebrating confusion without restoring action.
- **Lucky-result validation**: Treating one correct outcome as proof of knowledge.

## Worked Example

A team says a release must be “safe.” Instead of accepting examples, the examiner asks whether safe means zero incidents, bounded reversible harm, compliance, or a tested rollback path. Failed definitions reveal separate risk tolerances; the team adopts an operational threshold and a pilot.

## Key Takeaways

1. Stabilize decisive terms.
2. Preserve exposed unknowns.
3. Move from perplexity to evidence.
4. Review process separately from outcome luck.
