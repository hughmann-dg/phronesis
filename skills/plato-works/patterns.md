# Patterns — Plato Works

These are reconstructed decision patterns, not quotations or claims that Plato supplied a modern workflow.

## Definition Ladder

**When to use**: A decisive word shifts meaning across participants.

**How**: Ask for one account; distinguish examples from a definition; test scope, circularity, and counterexamples; adopt an operational definition if metaphysical agreement is unnecessary.

**Trade-offs**: Clarifies disagreement but can become semantic delay. (*Meno* 71a-79e; *Euthyphro* 5d-15e)

## Confidence-to-Evidence Audit

**When to use**: Status or fluency is standing in for knowledge.

**How**: State the claim, ask what the speaker knows directly, test boundaries, and identify what evidence would revise it.

**Trade-offs**: Counters false authority but must not flatten verified expertise into mere opinion. (*Apology* 21b-23b)

## Persuasion-Knowledge Split

**When to use**: A proposal is compelling but its audience cannot explain or test it.

**How**: Separate confidence change from competence change; request mechanism, evidence, and a transfer test.

**Trade-offs**: Exposes rhetoric but may undervalue legitimate concise communication. (*Gorgias* 454c-461b)

## Principle-Consistency Test

**When to use**: Pressure makes a convenient exception attractive.

**How**: Name the prior principle, test whether present reasons genuinely alter it, map obligations and harms, and examine what the exception authorizes elsewhere.

**Trade-offs**: Promotes integrity but must allow principles to be corrected and unjust duties to be rejected. (*Crito* 46b-54e)

## Aporia-to-Experiment

**When to use**: Examination has dissolved confidence without resolving action.

**How**: Preserve the exposed unknowns, select the highest-value question, and design a reversible test or evidence request.

**Trade-offs**: Converts doubt into learning; inappropriate when immediate safety action is required. (*Meno* 79e-86c, interpreted)
