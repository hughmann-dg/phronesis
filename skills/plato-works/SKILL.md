## Core Frameworks and Mental Models

### Define before evaluating

In *Meno*, Socrates repeatedly asks for what virtue is before accepting examples or claims about how it is acquired. Use definition requests when a decision turns on an unstable term, but permit operational definitions where an essence is unavailable. (*Meno* 71a-79e)

### Treat recognized confusion as progress

The movement from confident answers to perplexity exposes unsupported assumptions. Use aporia as a diagnostic stage, not as the endpoint of counsel. Follow it with evidence gathering or a bounded next step. (*Meno* 79e-86c)

### Test a definition against its consequences

*Euthyphro* examines candidate accounts of piety and tests whether they are circular, merely exemplary, or dependent on disputed approval. Apply the same checks to terms such as fairness, safety, or success. (*Euthyphro* 5d-15e)

### Separate wisdom from reputation for wisdom

In the *Apology*, Socratic examination reveals that social authority and confidence do not guarantee knowledge. Audit claims by evidence and scope while preserving respect for genuine expertise. (*Apology* 21b-23b)

### Separate persuasion from knowledge

*Gorgias* distinguishes belief-producing persuasion from instruction that yields knowledge. Ask whether a presentation changes understanding or merely confidence, and what competence the audience could demonstrate afterward. (*Gorgias* 454c-461b)

### Distinguish craft from gratification

Socrates challenges rhetoric as a practice that can optimize immediate approval without knowledge of the good it claims to serve. Test whether a method has an account of its objective, causal mechanism, and standards of correction. (*Gorgias* 462b-466a)

### Examine obligation through principle and relationship

The *Crito* tests escape against non-retaliation, consistency, and obligations to law and city. Use it to expose competing duties, not to demand obedience to unjust institutions. (*Crito* 49a-54e)

### Keep metaphysical argument separate from decision procedure

The *Phaedo* contains arguments about soul, recollection, forms, causation, and hypothesis. They are important to the volume but should not be silently converted into empirical claims or executable counsel. (*Phaedo* 70c-107b)

## Sources and Limits

Primary edition: Benjamin Jowett's third edition, *The Dialogues of Plato in Five Volumes*, Volume II, Oxford University Press, 1892, Project Gutenberg #76464 (`plato-dialogues`). Rights verification is scoped to the United States.

The edition includes Jowett's analyses, introductions, and marginal apparatus. Do not attribute that paratext to Plato. Dialogue speakers are dramatic participants, not automatically Plato's settled doctrine. Exact quotation requires checking the primary text and rendered locator; historical arguments do not override modern evidence, safety, rights, or specialist expertise.
