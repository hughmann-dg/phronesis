# Reference Unit 2: Earth Scroll

## Core Idea

The Earth scroll establishes strategy as a practiced craft: understand the whole field, allocate tools by fit, study scale, and learn rhythm rather than chasing ornament.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Carpenter-Leader Analogy**: Match material and people to work by demonstrated qualities.
  - When to use: Role, tool, or resource allocation.
  - How: Clarify the design, inspect available qualities, assign by fit, and retain even imperfect resources where their strength is useful.
  - Why it works / failure mode: It links architecture to allocation; it fails if people are reduced to materials or agency is ignored. ("Strategy Compared with Carpentry")
- **Five-Scroll Map**: Earth gives ground, Water flexible form, Fire contest and change, Wind comparison, and Void disciplined understanding.
  - When to use: Route a question without forcing one maxim onto the whole work. ("This Book Has Five Scrolls")
- **Tool-Fit Scan**: Compare tools by range, field, mobility, and operating conditions.
  - When to use: A team favors one method universally. ("Advantages of Weapons")
- **Rhythm Map**: Identify fitting, missing, and disrupting rhythms.
  - When to use: Sequencing, launch timing, negotiation cadence, or recovery. ("Rhythm in Strategy")

## Key Concepts

- **Strategy / 兵法**: The work's practiced way of individual and large-scale contest; modern use requires explicit transfer limits.
- **Craft**: Competence built through repeated work, not prestige or terminology.
- **Scale**: The relation between one-to-one and many-to-many situations.
- **Rhythm / 拍子**: Timing and cadence, including favorable and unfavorable fit.
- **Tool advantage**: Conditional usefulness rather than absolute superiority.

## Mental Models

- Use the carpenter analogy when a plan depends on fitting varied capabilities to one design.
- Treat timing as a sequence with transitions, not merely acting quickly.
- Study another craft to expose assumptions hidden by specialization.

## Anti-patterns

- **Display over substance**: Selling the appearance of a way without its practical result. ("The Way of Strategy")
- **Universal tool**: Treating one instrument or method as best in every field.
- **Scale leap**: Moving from a small example to a system conclusion without checking changed conditions.

## Worked Example

A migration team maps architects, operators, rollback tools, and environments as a carpenter maps design, materials, and tasks. It assigns by observed capability, rehearses the sequence, and defines the rhythm from preparation through recovery.

## Key Takeaways

1. Start with the whole design and actual materials.
2. Fit tools to conditions.
3. Treat rhythm as learnable through practice.
4. Check transfer between scales.
