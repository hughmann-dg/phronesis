# Reference Unit 3: Water Scroll

## Core Idea

The Water scroll joins composure, broad perception, unfixed posture, and repeated practice so form can fit the situation without becoming careless improvisation.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Practice-to-Own**: Convert written instruction into personally verified skill.
  - When to use: A plan is understood conceptually but untested operationally.
  - How: Examine details, rehearse them repeatedly, and test under representative pressure. (introduction)
- **Near-Far Gaze**: See distant matters as near and nearby matters in wider context.
  - When to use: Local signals and system effects must be held together.
  - How: Alternate detail inspection with broad field awareness; do not fixate. ("The Gaze in Strategy")
- **Attitude-No Attitude**: Do not make posture an end; configure it for the purpose it serves.
  - When to use: A team defends a process because it is standard.
  - How: State what the posture must enable, select the simplest fit, and change when conditions change. ("Attitude-No Attitude")

## Key Concepts

- **Water**: The scroll's model of form responsive to its container; this is an interpretive gloss, not permission to abandon constraints.
- **Composure**: Stable attention rather than passivity.
- **Gaze / 見付**: Perception across foreground and field.
- **Attitude / 構**: A posture or configuration serving action.
- **Embodied judgment**: Capability demonstrated through practice.

## Mental Models

- Use water as a fit metaphor when a stable purpose needs variable form.
- Think broad and detailed in alternating passes.
- Treat every stance as a hypothesis about what action it enables.

## Anti-patterns

- **Book mastery**: Confusing reading with competence.
- **Fixation**: Letting one urgent signal erase the wider field.
- **No-plan no-stance**: Mistaking flexibility for the absence of preparation or limits.

## Worked Example

A response team practices three incident scenarios. It holds the objective and safety rules fixed, varies roles and tools with conditions, and trains observers to alternate between the immediate alert and system-wide effects.

## Key Takeaways

1. Rehearsal turns a plan into tested capability.
2. Keep detail and field in view.
3. Posture serves purpose.
4. Flexibility requires preparation.
