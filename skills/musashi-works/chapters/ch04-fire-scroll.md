# Reference Unit 4: Fire Scroll

## Core Idea

The Fire scroll studies action amid opposition and change. For nonviolent decisions, its strongest transferable ideas are field awareness, initiative, transition points, deadlock recognition, and deliberate renewal.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Field Check**: Account for position, constraints, visibility, and routes before action. ("The Position of the Field")
- **Three Initiatives**: Distinguish initiating, receiving an initiative, and acting in a simultaneous transition.
  - When to use: Sequence and response posture materially affect outcomes.
  - How: Define observable behavior for each mode; avoid importing combat instructions. ("Three Initiatives")
- **Crossing-Point Test**: Identify a phase where conditions support decisive transition.
  - When to use: Migration, negotiation, launch, or exit has a costly middle state.
  - How: Set entry evidence, commitment action, fallback, and the cost of missing the point. ("Crossing at a Ford")
- **Deadlock Release**: Leave a mutually immobilizing pattern instead of pushing harder by habit. ("Releasing Four Hands")
- **Mountain-Sea Change**: After an approach has failed repeatedly, change the class of approach rather than repeat cosmetic variations. ("Mountain-Sea Change")
- **Renewal**: Reframe and restart when entanglement prevents useful action. ("Renewal")

## Key Concepts

- **Initiative / 先**: Relationship between acting and responding.
- **Crossing**: A decisive transition with timing and position constraints.
- **Deadlock**: Repetition that preserves the contest but not progress.
- **Renewal**: A material reset of frame or method.
- **Collapse**: Loss of coherence that may make continuation ineffective. ("Knowing Collapse")

## Mental Models

- Treat a transition like a crossing: conditions, commitment, exposure, and exit all matter.
- If two retries preserve the same causal mechanism, the approach has not materially changed.
- Set renewal triggers before sunk costs distort judgment.

## Anti-patterns

- **Speed worship**: Acting quickly without reading the transition.
- **Cosmetic variation**: Renaming the same failed method.
- **Adversarial projection**: Treating colleagues or customers as enemies.

## Worked Example

A product migration defines its crossing point as completion of a tested rollback, verified data parity, and staffed support. A second failure caused by the same dependency triggers a different migration architecture rather than a third cosmetic retry.

## Key Takeaways

1. Read the field before acting.
2. Make transition criteria observable.
3. Recognize deadlock early.
4. Renew the method when the causal pattern persists.
