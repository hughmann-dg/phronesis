# Reference Unit 7: Executable Limits

## Core Idea

*Go Rin no Sho* is a historical martial-strategy text. Modern decision use must extract bounded, nonviolent patterns without treating combat success as general moral or empirical authority.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Nonviolent Transfer Test**: State the source concept, proposed analogue, changed assumptions, affected stakeholders, and element that does not transfer.
- **Practice Safety Gate**: Rehearsal must remain lawful, consensual, reversible where possible, and safe for participants and systems.
- **Adversarial-Framing Gate**: Use contest models only where incentives genuinely conflict; do not manufacture enemies in cooperative settings.

## Key Concepts

- **Interpretive doctrine**: Project-authored principles linked to source locators.
- **Behavioral procedure**: Executable counsel that remains subordinate to rights and safety.
- **Historical claim**: A statement needing evidence beyond Musashi's own account.

## Anti-patterns

- **Violence transfer**: Turning sword technique into operational guidance for harm.
- **Outcome moralism**: Treating winning as proof that an action is right.
- **Persona authority**: Advising "as Musashi" instead of exposing reasons and limits.
- **Enemy inflation**: Framing partners, employees, or customers as opponents.

## Worked Example

A team uses the crossing metaphor for a database migration. It defines evidence, rollback, and staffing for the transition while discarding the combat assumptions and preserving safety and stakeholder duties.

## Key Takeaways

1. Transfer patterns, not violence.
2. Rights and safety override tactical fit.
3. Historical assertions need independent evidence.
4. The source does not settle moral legitimacy.
