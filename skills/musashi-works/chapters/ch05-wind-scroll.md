# Reference Unit 5: Wind Scroll

## Core Idea

The Wind scroll learns through comparison, criticizing schools that make one feature—size, strength, shortness, many techniques, fixed posture, gaze, footwork, speed, or secrecy—the essence of strategy.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **One-Sidedness Audit**: Find the variable a method treats as universally decisive.
  - When to use: A doctrine, vendor, or team claims one advantage solves every case.
  - How: List its preferred variable, identify conditions where it helps, then test cases where another variable dominates. (Wind scroll, named critiques)
- **No Secret Shortcut**: Treat labels of inner/outer or secret/public cautiously when they substitute for demonstrated understanding.
  - When to use: Expertise is defended through inaccessible jargon rather than evidence. ("Outer and Inner in Other Schools")
- **Speed-Is-Not-Rhythm**: Separate raw haste from timing fitted to conditions.
  - When to use: A plan equates faster with better. ("Speed in Other Schools")

## Key Concepts

- **Wind / 風**: Also carries the sense of style or school in this scroll.
- **Comparative study**: Learning one's own method by examining alternatives.
- **One-sidedness**: Overcommitment to a single dimension.
- **Secret teaching**: A claimed privileged layer whose operational value still requires evidence.

## Mental Models

- Use comparison to reveal hidden defaults in your own method.
- Ask what condition would reverse the claimed advantage.
- Prefer demonstrated capability over ornamental taxonomy.

## Anti-patterns

- **Straw-school critique**: Treating Musashi's description of rivals as neutral history.
- **Universal anti-form**: Turning criticism of fixed methods into a new fixed rule against standardization.
- **Mystique as evidence**: Accepting secrecy or lineage as proof of effectiveness.

## Worked Example

A team compares two deployment methods. Instead of declaring one universally safer, it tests size, time pressure, observability, reversibility, and operator skill, then states which conditions favor each.

## Key Takeaways

1. Comparison reveals attachment.
2. Every advantage is conditional.
3. Speed and rhythm are different.
4. Critiques of other schools are interested claims, not neutral history.
