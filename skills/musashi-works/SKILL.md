## Core Frameworks and Mental Models

### Practice precedes dependable judgment

The Water introduction says the written account cannot by itself confer mastery and repeatedly directs the student to examine and practice. Use rehearsal under representative conditions before trusting a plan that works only on paper. (Water scroll, introduction)

### Read rhythm, including disrupted rhythm

The Earth scroll treats rhythm as present in arts, occupations, rise and decline, and strategy. It distinguishes fitting rhythm from rhythm that misses or disrupts. Map preparation, opening, commitment, follow-through, and interruption instead of reducing timing to speed. (Earth scroll, "Rhythm in Strategy")

### Fit tools and posture to conditions

The Earth scroll compares weapons by circumstance; the Water scroll rejects a fixed posture detached from its purpose; the Wind scroll criticizes one-sided attachment to size, strength, speed, footwork, gaze, and secret technique. Choose configurations by task and evidence, not prestige or habit. (Earth scroll, "Advantages of Weapons"; Water scroll, "Attitude-No Attitude"; Wind scroll)

### Connect small and large scales cautiously

The work repeatedly asks the reader to study one-person and many-person strategy together and to move between broad and detailed perception. Transfer a pattern only after checking what changes with scale. (Earth scroll, "Meaning of Strategy"; Water scroll, introduction and "The Gaze in Strategy")

### Recognize the crossing point

The Fire scroll's ford metaphor concerns a phase where conditions, position, and timing make a decisive transition possible. Define the evidence for that point and the action it triggers; do not drift through an expensive change. (Fire scroll, "Crossing at a Ford")

### Break deadlock and renew

The Fire scroll advises disengaging when both sides are locked, changing the approach after a repeated method fails, and renewing one's spirit when entanglement prevents progress. Use explicit stop and switch criteria rather than variation for its own sake. (Fire scroll, "Releasing Four Hands," "Mountain-Sea Change," and "Renewal")

### Do not confuse uncertainty with void

The Void scroll distinguishes lack of understanding or confusion from the disciplined clarity it calls void. In modern use, unknowns remain unknowns; do not spiritualize missing evidence. (Void scroll)
