## Core Frameworks and Mental Models

### Compare conditions before commitment

Chapter I assesses moral cohesion, environment, terrain, command, and method, then compares the parties through concrete questions. Use a pre-commitment scorecard to find decisive uncertainty, not to manufacture false precision. (I.1-18)

### Price delay and prolonged contest

Chapter II emphasizes the compounding cost of extended operations. Model burn, attention, morale, opportunity cost, and the point at which persistence undermines the objective. (II.1-19)

### Prefer objective achievement over destructive contest

Chapter III ranks preserving and subduing above ruinous direct attack and treats knowledge of self and opponent as a condition of sound action. Seek changes in incentives, coalition, information, or position before escalation. (III.1-18)

### Separate preparation from opportunity

Chapter IV makes secure disposition something one can prepare while the exploitable opening depends partly on the other side. Improve resilience without pretending to control when opportunity appears. (IV.1-20)

### Combine expected and unexpected methods

Chapter V treats direct and indirect methods, combinations, momentum, and disciplined organization as sources of force. Establish a reliable baseline, then reserve variation that changes the other side's expectations. (V.1-23)

### Shape weak and strong points

Chapter VI emphasizes arriving prepared, making the other party respond, avoiding concentration, and adapting form. Diagnose where commitments create exposed dependencies and where optionality creates leverage. (VI.1-34)

### Treat intelligence as an operating system

Chapter XIII makes foreknowledge depend on people and organized source handling rather than divination. Separate local, internal, converted, doomed, and surviving agents as historical categories; in modern use, require legality, consent, source protection, and corroboration. (XIII.1-27)
