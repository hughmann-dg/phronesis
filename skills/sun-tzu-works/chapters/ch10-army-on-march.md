# Reference Unit 10: Chapter IX — The Army on the March

## Core Idea

Chapter IX combines situational positioning with interpretation of environmental and behavioral signs, while warning that discipline and humane treatment must work together.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Signal-Hypothesis Table**: Treat an observed sign as evidence for several possible explanations. (IX)
  - When to use: Sparse behavior is read as intent.
  - How: List hypotheses, diagnostic observations, and confidence.
- **Words-Action Consistency Check**: Compare conciliatory or forceful language with actual preparation and movement. (IX, later paragraphs)
  - When to use: Public messaging conflicts with behavior.
  - How: Weight costly action more than cheap statement while preserving alternatives.
- **Care-Discipline Pair**: Cohesion requires both treatment and enforceable standards. (IX, final paragraphs)

## Key Concepts

- **Sign**: Observable indicator whose interpretation remains uncertain.
- **Positioning**: Contextual choice of place and movement.
- **Discipline**: Reliable adherence supported by prior relationship and clarity.

## Anti-patterns

- **One-signal certainty**: Treating an ambiguous cue as proof.
- **Mind reading**: Inferring intent without alternatives.

## Worked Example

A supplier becomes slow to answer. Instead of assuming bad faith, the team considers overload, internal approval, cash distress, and strategic delay, then requests delivery evidence and activates a fallback.

## Key Takeaways

1. Generate competing explanations.
2. Seek diagnostic evidence.
3. Compare words with costly action.
4. Pair standards with relationship.
