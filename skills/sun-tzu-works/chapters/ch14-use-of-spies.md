# Reference Unit 14: Chapter XIII — The Use of Spies

## Core Idea

Chapter XIII treats foreknowledge as a product of organized human sources, classifies source relationships, and emphasizes handling, secrecy, conversion, and corroboration in an adversarial setting.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Source-Type Map**: Distinguish access paths and incentives rather than treating “intelligence” as one thing. (XIII.9-15)
  - When to use: A critical claim comes from an interested source.
  - How: Record access, motive, independence, reliability, and risk.
- **Converted-Source Corroboration**: A source connected to the counterpart can inform and mislead. (XIII.16-25)
  - When to use: Insider information drives a decision.
  - How: Seek independent confirmation and model deception.
- **Human Foreknowledge Boundary**: Information comes through observable sources, not prophecy. (XIII.3-7)

## Key Concepts

- **Five spies**: Local, inward, converted, doomed, and surviving categories in Giles (XIII.9-15).
- **Foreknowledge**: Advance knowledge through human inquiry.
- **Source handling**: Protection, incentives, coordination, and verification.

## Anti-patterns

- **Surveillance romanticism**: Using historical espionage to excuse privacy invasion.
- **Single-insider certainty**: Ignoring manipulation or error.

## Worked Example

A potential partner's former employee alleges financial distress. The team checks consent and legality, treats the claim as interested testimony, corroborates with filings and delivery behavior, and protects the person's identity.

## Key Takeaways

1. Map access and incentives.
2. Corroborate interested sources.
3. Protect people and legality.
4. Reject supernatural certainty.
