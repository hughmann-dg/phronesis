# Reference Unit 6: Chapter V — Energy

## Core Idea

Chapter V links organization, signaling, combinations of direct and indirect methods, momentum, and use of collective configuration rather than dependence on isolated heroics.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Scale Through Structure**: Large groups become manageable through subdivision, roles, and signals. (V.1-4)
  - When to use: Coordination load overwhelms leaders.
  - How: Clarify units, interfaces, decision rights, and feedback.
- **Baseline-Variation Pair**: Combine reliable normal operations with less expected variation. (V.5-11)
  - When to use: Predictability enables a counterpart to adapt.
  - How: Keep safety-critical routines stable and vary only bounded dimensions.
- **Configuration over Heroics**: Design conditions so ordinary execution produces force. (V.15-23)

## Key Concepts

- **Direct/indirect**: Giles's rendering of paired methods; translation is context-dependent.
- **Energy**: Edition term for collective effect and momentum.
- **Signals**: Coordination mechanisms.

## Anti-patterns

- **Surprise addiction**: Varying constantly until one's own team is confused.
- **Hero dependency**: Using exceptional effort to compensate for poor design.

## Worked Example

A support organization standardizes triage and escalation, then forms a temporary cross-functional cell for novel incidents. Stable routing preserves reliability while bounded variation handles exceptions.

## Key Takeaways

1. Scale through structure and signals.
2. Keep the baseline reliable.
3. Vary with purpose.
4. Design systems that reduce heroic dependence.
