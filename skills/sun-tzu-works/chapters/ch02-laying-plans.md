# Reference Unit 2: Chapter I — Laying Plans

## Core Idea

Chapter I compares conditions before commitment and treats calculation, adaptation, and concealment as elements of adversarial advantage.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Five-Factor Assessment**: Compare cohesion, environment, terrain, command, and method. (I.3-13)
  - When to use: Before committing to a costly contest.
  - How: Record evidence, unknowns, and which factor can change.
- **Comparative Questions**: Evaluate relative alignment, capability, discipline, strength, training, and incentives. (I.13-18)
  - When to use: A plan is assessed in isolation.
  - How: Compare against the actual alternative or counterpart.

## Key Concepts

- **Moral law**: Giles's edition-specific rendering of cohesion/alignment (I.4).
- **Heaven and Earth**: Temporal/environmental and spatial conditions (I.5-6).
- **Method and discipline**: Organization, roles, and logistics (I.10).
- **Deception**: Adversarial misdirection discussed at I.18-26.

## Anti-patterns

- **Scorecard theatre**: Assigning numbers without evidence.
- **Deception default**: Extending wartime concealment to ordinary stakeholders.

## Worked Example

Before entering a price war, a firm compares customer loyalty, runway, channel constraints, operating discipline, and competitor incentives. Weak evidence on competitor costs becomes a research item, not an invented score.

## Key Takeaways

1. Compare conditions before action.
2. Preserve uncertainty in the assessment.
3. Test assumptions against a counterpart.
4. Subject concealment to ethical review.
