# Reference Unit 3: Chapter II — Waging War

## Core Idea

Chapter II emphasizes the resource, logistical, and political damage of prolonged operations and the importance of achieving the objective without self-exhaustion.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Duration-Cost Curve**: Model how time compounds expense, fatigue, and lost opportunity. (II.1-6)
  - When to use: A campaign has no exit condition.
  - How: Track burn, attention, morale, opportunity cost, and failure trigger.
- **Objective-Cost Consistency**: Ensure the means do not consume the value sought. (II.7-19)
  - When to use: Persistence is praised independently of results.
  - How: Restate the objective and compare marginal progress with marginal cost.

## Key Concepts

- **Protraction**: Extended contest whose burdens accumulate.
- **Logistics**: Material support connecting action to sustainability.
- **Tempo**: Rate of action, valuable only relative to preparedness and objective.

## Anti-patterns

- **Speed worship**: Moving quickly without readiness.
- **Sunk-cost persistence**: Continuing because much has already been spent.

## Worked Example

A litigation-like vendor dispute drains leadership attention. The team sets a two-week evidence window and settlement threshold, then compares continued conflict with switching cost and operational risk.

## Key Takeaways

1. Put duration on the cost model.
2. Define a stop or redesign trigger.
3. Reject means that destroy the objective's value.
4. Distinguish tempo from haste.
