# Reference Unit 13: Chapter XII — The Attack by Fire

## Core Idea

Chapter XII presents fire attack as conditional on means, weather, timing, and observed effects, then warns against action driven merely by anger and insists that gain must justify movement.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Means-Conditions-Timing Gate**: A tactic works only when enabling conditions align. (XII.1-15)
  - When to use: A powerful intervention is proposed as universally effective.
  - How: List prerequisites, verification signals, propagation risk, and abort condition.
- **Emotion-to-Objective Pause**: Anger is transient, while consequences may be irreversible. (XII.16-22)
  - When to use: Retaliation or urgency drives escalation.
  - How: Restate the objective, wait if safe, and require evidence of net advantage.

## Key Concepts

- **Fire attack**: Historical destructive tactic; only its conditional reasoning transfers safely.
- **Response observation**: Adaptation based on actual effect.
- **Anger restraint**: Warning against irreversible action from temporary emotion.

## Anti-patterns

- **Literal transfer**: Using this source for harmful operational guidance.
- **Retaliatory escalation**: Acting to satisfy anger rather than objective.

## Worked Example

After a public criticism, a company considers immediate legal threats. The team pauses, models amplification risk, clarifies the correction objective, and chooses a factual response plus escalation trigger.

## Key Takeaways

1. Transfer conditional reasoning, not destructive tactics.
2. Verify enabling conditions.
3. Observe effects before compounding action.
4. Separate anger from objective.
