# Reference Unit 8: Chapter VII — Manoeuvring

## Core Idea

Chapter VII examines the difficulty of converting strategic intent into coordinated movement amid route trade-offs, fatigue, dispersed forces, communication limits, and changing morale.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Route-Cost Trade-off**: An indirect route may create advantage, but forced speed can fragment capability. (VII.1-12)
  - When to use: A shortcut imposes hidden operational cost.
  - How: Compare time, cohesion, load, and arrival readiness.
- **Shared-Signal Design**: Coordination requires visible and audible signals suited to conditions. (VII.23-28)
  - When to use: Distributed groups interpret priorities differently.
  - How: Define channels, ownership, acknowledgments, and conflict resolution.
- **Morale/Tempo Awareness**: Capacity varies through a cycle; timing affects effectiveness. (VII.29-35)

## Key Concepts

- **Manoeuvre**: Converting position into coordinated action.
- **Enticement**: Historical adversarial device requiring ethical limits.
- **Signals**: Common coordination system.

## Anti-patterns

- **Arrival without readiness**: Meeting a date with exhausted or incomplete capability.
- **Signal proliferation**: Multiple channels with no authority rule.

## Worked Example

A migration is accelerated by parallel workstreams, but integration and on-call readiness lag. The plan changes the target from earliest cutover to earliest cutover with verified rollback and staffed response.

## Key Takeaways

1. Price the route, not only the destination.
2. Preserve cohesion under speed.
3. Establish shared signals.
4. Match timing to real capacity.
