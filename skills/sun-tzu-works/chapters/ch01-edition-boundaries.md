# Reference Unit 1: Edition Boundaries

## Core Idea

Giles's 1910 edition interleaves translation, Chinese text, traditional commentaries, translator notes, historical examples, and criticism. Accurate use requires attribution at paragraph level.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Text-Layer Check**: Identify whether a claim is translated Sun Tzu, Giles, or a named commentator.
  - When to use: A source claim appears in surrounding notes.
  - How: Locate the numbered paragraph, then read its attribution and formatting.
- **Transfer Boundary**: Reframe military analysis only after legality, rights, safety, and factual relevance are checked.
  - When to use: Applying the text outside armed conflict.
  - How: State the nonviolent analogue and the element that does not transfer.

## Key Concepts

- **Numbered paragraph**: Giles's cross-reference system within each chapter.
- **Commentary**: Traditional interpretation quoted or summarized by Giles.
- **Translator note**: Giles's philological or historical analysis.
- **Concordance**: Edition apparatus, not part of the treatise.

## Anti-patterns

- **Layer collapse**: Attributing a commentator's maxim to Sun Tzu.
- **Warfare metaphor inflation**: Treating ordinary cooperation as combat.
- **Translation blending**: Mixing modern terms with Giles without disclosure.

## Worked Example

A positioning memo cites an idea found in Giles's note to Chapter VI. The writer labels it as Giles's interpretation, cites the chapter/paragraph, and uses current evidence before applying it to vendor negotiation.

## Key Takeaways

1. Retrieve before quoting.
2. Attribute each textual layer.
3. Cite chapter and numbered paragraph.
4. State transfer limits.
