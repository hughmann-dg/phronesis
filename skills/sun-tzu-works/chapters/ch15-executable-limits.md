# Reference Unit 15: Executable Limits

## Core Idea

Sun Tzu's strategic concepts can inform nonviolent positioning only after translation, attribution, factual validation, and independent ethical constraints. Effectiveness is never sufficient permission.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Nonviolent Transfer Test**: State the abstract relation being transferred and exclude violent mechanics.
  - When to use: Applying military language to business or personal decisions.
  - How: Name objective, actors, legitimate moves, protected interests, and prohibited methods.
- **Legality-Rights Gate**: Review deception, surveillance, coercion, privacy, discrimination, and contractual duties independently.
  - When to use: A tactic depends on hidden information or pressure.
  - How: Stop execution until authorized ethical and legal review is complete.
- **Evidence Gate**: Treat historical examples and commentator claims as hypotheses, not modern causal evidence.

## Key Concepts

- **Positioning**: Shaping information, timing, options, and dependencies before commitment.
- **Optionality**: Preserved ability to choose later, with a cost and expiry.
- **Hard stop**: Constraint that effectiveness cannot override.
- **Deferral**: Routing to rights, safety, legal, or specialist analysis.

## Anti-patterns

- **Everyone-is-an-enemy framing**: Destroying cooperative possibilities.
- **Ancient-authority proof**: Replacing evidence with prestige.
- **Cleverness license**: Excusing dishonesty because it seems strategic.
- **Indefinite maneuvering**: Avoiding accountable commitment.

## Worked Example

A negotiator considers withholding a known product safety issue to preserve leverage. The legality-rights gate stops the tactic; safety and truthful disclosure duties override positional advantage.

## Key Takeaways

1. Transfer relationships, not violence.
2. Attribute Giles and commentators accurately.
3. Apply legal and ethical hard stops.
4. Require current evidence.
5. Put expiry triggers on optionality.
