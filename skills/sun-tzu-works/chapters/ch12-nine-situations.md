# Reference Unit 12: Chapter XI — The Nine Situations

## Core Idea

Chapter XI maps different forms of strategic ground to cohesion, commitment, supply, movement, alliance, and command, emphasizing action appropriate to position rather than universal tactics.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Situation-Commitment Map**: Assess how reversible the position is and how incentives affect cohesion. (XI.1-14)
  - When to use: A team assumes all participants share commitment.
  - How: Map exit options, exposure, dependence, and common interest.
- **Contextual Action Rule**: Change coordination and risk posture with position. (XI.15-54)
  - When to use: Policy ignores stage or stakes.
  - How: Specify which contextual variable justifies the variation.
- **Commitment-Device Review**: High-stakes conditions can intensify effort, but manufactured entrapment is ethically dangerous. (XI.27-45)

## Key Concepts

- **Nine situations**: Chapter XI's contextual ground categories, distinct from Chapter X's six terrains.
- **Serious ground**: Position with deeper commitment and supply implications.
- **Desperate ground**: Historical no-exit condition, not a management technique.

## Anti-patterns

- **Burn-the-boats management**: Removing options to force performance.
- **Category literalism**: Mapping modern contexts to ancient labels without evidence.

## Worked Example

A pilot team and a company-wide migration face different reversibility. The pilot keeps optionality; the migration requires redundant support and clear cutover authority, without artificially trapping staff.

## Key Takeaways

1. Map reversibility and dependence.
2. Adapt coordination to stakes.
3. Do not manufacture desperation.
4. Keep Chapter X and XI classifications distinct.
