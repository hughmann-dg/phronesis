# Reference Unit 7: Chapter VI — Weak Points and Strong

## Core Idea

Chapter VI treats initiative, concentration, dispersion, responsiveness, and formless adaptation as ways of shaping where relative strength appears.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Initiative-by-Value**: Make the counterpart respond by presenting something they must address. (VI.1-8)
  - When to use: Your plan is purely reactive.
  - How: Identify a legitimate offer, deadline, dependency, or information move that changes choices.
- **Concentration Audit**: Strength in one place implies exposure elsewhere. (VI.9-22)
  - When to use: A competitor or organization appears strong everywhere.
  - How: Map finite resources, commitments, and response time.
- **Adaptive Form**: Avoid repeating a successful configuration after conditions change. (VI.23-34)

## Key Concepts

- **Weak/strong**: Relative, contextual position rather than fixed trait.
- **Concentration**: Focus of limited resources.
- **Formlessness**: Adaptability that resists easy prediction, not absence of discipline.

## Anti-patterns

- **Manipulative bait**: Creating harmful or deceptive pressure.
- **Static playbook**: Repeating yesterday's advantage.

## Worked Example

A small entrant cannot match an incumbent across all accounts. It concentrates on a regulated niche the incumbent's general workflow serves poorly, while keeping product architecture adaptable.

## Key Takeaways

1. Treat strength as relative.
2. Find the exposure created by concentration.
3. Create legitimate initiative.
4. Reassess after conditions change.
