# Patterns — Sun Tzu Works

These are nonviolent decision reconstructions, not blanket transfers of military conduct.

## Comparative Position Scan

**When to use**: Before a costly commitment.

**How**: Compare objective alignment, environmental conditions, route constraints, leadership quality, operating discipline, capacity, and incentives; mark unknowns rather than scoring invented numbers.

**Trade-offs**: Improves preparation but can encourage overconfidence if comparison data are weak. (I.1-18)

## Avoid-the-Siege Move

**When to use**: A direct contest is expensive and the objective can be achieved indirectly.

**How**: Identify the real objective; map alliances, dependencies, information, and timing; look for a move that changes the contest before escalating.

**Trade-offs**: Preserves resources, but delay is harmful when duties require direct action. (III.1-10)

## Secure-Then-Wait

**When to use**: Opportunity timing is outside your control.

**How**: Reduce avoidable failure modes, pre-commit a trigger, and preserve capacity to act when evidence changes.

**Trade-offs**: Builds resilience but can become indefinite maneuvering. (IV.1-10)

## Expectation Break

**When to use**: A counterpart has adapted to a predictable baseline.

**How**: Keep reliable operations stable; vary sequence, channel, or timing without violating truthfulness or commitments.

**Trade-offs**: Gains surprise but complexity can damage coordination. (V.5-23)

## Source Portfolio

**When to use**: A decision depends on contested external facts.

**How**: Separate source access, incentives, independence, and corroboration; protect consent and legality; identify disinformation risk.

**Trade-offs**: Improves information but creates privacy, safety, and bias risks. (XIII, adapted)
