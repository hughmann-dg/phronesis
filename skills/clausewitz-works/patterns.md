# Patterns

## Objective-Means Trace

**When to use**: A complex plan has accumulated workstreams, metrics, and dependencies.

**How**:
1. State the governing objective and termination condition.
2. Trace each major means to that objective.
3. Mark means that have become goals in themselves.
4. Identify the decisive dependency and the evidence supporting it.
5. Remove, defer, or subordinate activity that does not advance the objective.

**Trade-offs**: Concentration improves coherence but can create single-point risk; retain resilience and legal/ethical constraints. (I.1; III.1; VIII.6)

## Friction Budget

**When to use**: A plan succeeds only under coordinated, timely execution.

**How**:
1. List communication, timing, staffing, information, tooling, and decision impediments.
2. Model several small failures occurring together.
3. Add slack, rehearsal, fallback paths, and local authority.
4. Define indicators that trigger adaptation.
5. Run a limited trial under realistic conditions.

**Trade-offs**: Slack costs resources; zero slack hides rather than removes uncertainty. (I.7)

## Culmination Gate

**When to use**: Momentum encourages continued expansion or commitment.

**How**:
1. Define the benefit and cost of one more increment.
2. Track distance from support, coordination burden, resistance, fatigue, and opportunity cost.
3. Set stop, consolidate, and withdraw thresholds before launch.
4. Reassess after success as rigorously as after failure.

**Trade-offs**: Stopping early may leave value unrealized; stopping late can reverse prior gains. (VII.5, VII.22)

## Information Reliability Matrix

**When to use**: Conflicting reports drive a time-sensitive decision.

**How**:
1. Separate observation, inference, and rumor.
2. Score source access, incentives, recency, corroboration, and decision relevance.
3. Identify which uncertainty can be reduced in time.
4. Choose a reversible action where decisive evidence is weak.

**Trade-offs**: More evidence costs time; action under uncertainty requires explicit downside containment. (I.6; II.5-6)

## Defence-to-Action Transition

**When to use**: A team can delay or preserve position but must eventually pursue a positive objective.

**How**:
1. Name what defence protects and for how long.
2. Use time to improve information, position, support, and readiness.
3. Define the condition that justifies transition to positive action.
4. Recheck culmination and termination conditions.

**Trade-offs**: Defence can preserve options or become indefinite avoidance. (VI.1-5; VII.1)
