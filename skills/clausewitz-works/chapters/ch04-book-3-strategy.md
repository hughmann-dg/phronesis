# Reference Unit 4: Book III — Strategy in General

## Core Idea

Strategy coordinates engagements toward the governing objective while accounting for moral forces, surprise, concentration, time, reserves, economy, and pauses in action.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Engagement-to-Objective Trace**: Strategy links each engagement or major commitment to the larger purpose. (III.1)
  - When to use: Teams optimize local wins that may not combine into strategic progress.
  - How: Map each commitment to objective, dependency, sequence, and termination.
- **Assembly in Space and Time**: Concentrate what must act together while respecting coordination and timing. (III.11-12)
  - When to use: Resources are dispersed across priorities.
  - How: Identify the decision point, co-required capabilities, timing window, and nondecisive work.
- **Reserve-and-Economy Review**: Withhold flexibility only for genuine uncertainty; do not idle decisive capacity without reason. (III.13-14)
  - When to use: Choosing between full commitment and retained option value.
  - How: Specify what uncertainty the reserve covers and when it will be released.

## Key Concepts

- **Strategy**: Use of engagements for the war's object. (III.1)
- **Moral forces**: Human and organizational qualities shaping action. (III.3-5)
- **Surprise**: Advantage limited by secrecy, speed, and friction. (III.9)
- **Strategic reserve**: Contested concept of capacity kept for later strategic use. (III.13)
- **Economy of forces**: Avoiding waste of available capacity. (III.14)

## Mental Models

- Use concentration to make a decision possible, not merely to maximize activity.
- Treat reserve as an explicit response to uncertainty, not vague caution.

## Anti-patterns

- **Everything is priority**: Dispersion disguised as ambition.
- **Reserve without trigger**: Capacity withheld indefinitely.
- **Surprise fantasy**: Assuming secrecy removes execution difficulty.

## Worked Example

A transformation program funds ten initiatives equally. The objective depends chiefly on identity migration, training, and customer continuity during one window. The assembly test concentrates these co-required capabilities, pauses ornamental work, and holds a small incident reserve with clear release triggers. (III.11-14)

## Key Takeaways

1. Trace local commitments to the governing objective.
2. Concentrate co-required capabilities at the decision point.
3. Give reserves a named uncertainty and trigger.
4. Account for human and organizational forces.
