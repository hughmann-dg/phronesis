# Reference Unit 3: Book II — On the Theory of War

## Core Idea

Theory should organize study and sharpen judgment rather than prescribe mechanical rules for a domain governed by interaction, uncertainty, and context.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Theory-as-Education**: Use theory to illuminate relations and train judgment, not replace it. (II.2-4)
  - When to use: A framework is being applied as an automatic checklist.
  - How: Name assumptions, compare context, test exceptions, and retain accountable judgment.
- **Critical Analysis Loop**: Distinguish facts, means, intentions, results, and plausible alternatives when studying a case. (II.5)
  - When to use: Learning from success or failure.
  - How: Reconstruct what was known at the time, analyze causal links, and avoid hindsight certainty.
- **Historical-Example Gate**: Examples explain principles only when context and causal relevance are established. (II.6)
  - When to use: An analogy is doing most of the argumentative work.
  - How: Compare objective, technology, institutions, incentives, information, and alternatives.

## Key Concepts

- **Theory**: Structured aid to inquiry. (II.2)
- **Methodicism**: Repeated method useful only within bounded conditions. (II.4)
- **Criticism**: Examination of means, ends, and evidence. (II.5)
- **Historical example**: Case used to explain or support a proposition. (II.6)
- **Hindsight**: Information available to the critic but not the original decision-maker.

## Mental Models

- Use theories as maps with scale and scope, not autopilots.
- Reconstruct the decision-time information set before judging execution.

## Anti-patterns

- **Checklist sovereignty**: Allowing a method to make the decision.
- **Outcome bias**: Treating success as proof of sound reasoning.
- **Decorative history**: Naming a famous case without causal comparison.

## Worked Example

Clausewitz's critical method asks the analyst to examine both the means selected and the information available. For a failed migration, review the evidence and alternatives visible before launch, then distinguish flawed judgment from unforeseeable events. The goal is improved future judgment, not retrospective omniscience. (II.5-6)

## Key Takeaways

1. Theory educates judgment; it does not abolish it.
2. Separate decision quality from outcome luck.
3. Compare historical context before transferring a lesson.
4. Make causal reasoning explicit.
