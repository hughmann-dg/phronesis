# Reference Unit 6: Book V — Military Forces

## Core Idea

Book V makes execution concrete through force relationships, organization, movement, subsistence, bases, communications, and terrain; strategy without these conditions is aspiration.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Capability-System Map**: Treat execution capacity as an interacting system of people, organization, movement, supply, communication, and place. (V.1-18)
  - When to use: A plan reduces capability to headcount or budget.
  - How: Map components, throughput, bottlenecks, ownership, and recovery.
- **Base-and-Line Dependency**: Operating reach depends on support bases and communication lines. (V.15-16)
  - When to use: Work expands far from expertise, infrastructure, or decision authority.
  - How: Identify critical support nodes, latency, failure isolation, and alternative routes.
- **Movement Cost Review**: Repositioning capacity consumes time, energy, coordination, and readiness. (V.10-12)
  - When to use: A roadmap assumes teams or systems can switch instantly.
  - How: Price handoff, context rebuild, sequencing, fatigue, and unavailable overlap.

## Key Concepts

- **Relation of power**: Comparative capacity, not an isolated quantity. (V.3)
- **Order and disposition**: Arrangement for coordinated action. (V.5-6)
- **Marches**: Movement with cumulative operational cost. (V.10-12)
- **Subsistence**: Sustaining force through supply. (V.14)
- **Base and communication**: Support and connection required for action. (V.15-16)

## Mental Models

- Think of support capacity as part of the product, not overhead outside the strategy.
- Use communication latency as a limit on safe operational reach.

## Anti-patterns

- **Headcount arithmetic**: Assuming equal numbers imply equal capability.
- **Instant redeployment**: Ignoring context and transition cost.
- **Support invisibility**: Scaling the front line while starving logistics.

## Worked Example

A distributed program doubles delivery teams but centralizes approvals, incident expertise, and data access in one small group. The capability-system map predicts congestion despite higher headcount. The plan scales support nodes, delegates bounded authority, and tests communication failure before expansion. (V.3, V.15-16)

## Key Takeaways

1. Model capability as a system.
2. Include support, movement, and communication in capacity estimates.
3. Price redeployment explicitly.
4. Protect alternative support paths.
