# Reference Unit 7: Book VI — Defence

## Core Idea

Defence benefits from time, position, support, and the burdens placed on attack, but it remains purposeful only when connected to preservation, counteraction, or a later positive aim.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Defensive-Advantage Inventory**: Identify time, position, local support, information, and burden shifted to the advancing actor. (VI.1-6)
  - When to use: Deciding whether to hold, delay, withdraw, or counteract.
  - How: Name what is protected, duration, resource flow, learning gained, and transition conditions.
- **Layered Resistance Plan**: Avoid relying on one position or barrier; use successive resistance and preservation of capacity. (VI.8, VI.27-30)
  - When to use: A single control or deadline is treated as the whole defense.
  - How: Build detection, containment, fallback, recovery, and decision layers.
- **Centre-of-Gravity Hypothesis**: Identify the connection on which coherence depends, then treat it as a testable hypothesis. (VI.27)
  - When to use: A system appears to have one decisive dependency.
  - How: Map coupling, substitution, resilience, and effects of disruption without targeting people or civilian systems.

## Key Concepts

- **Defensive strength**: Advantages arising from waiting and position. (VI.1-5)
- **Resistance**: Action that imposes cost and preserves options. (VI.8)
- **Fortress/position**: One component of a wider defensive system. (VI.10-14)
- **Retreat into the interior**: Trading space for time and burden under specific historical conditions. (VI.25)
- **Theatre defence**: Coordinated defence across a larger system. (VI.27-30)

## Mental Models

- Use time only when it improves position, information, or support.
- Think in layers rather than single barriers.

## Anti-patterns

- **Passive defence**: Waiting without accumulating advantage.
- **Single-wall security**: Treating one control as resilience.
- **Centre-of-gravity certainty**: Declaring one decisive point without evidence.

## Worked Example

A service facing a risky migration keeps the current system temporarily. Defence is strategic only if the time funds observability, rehearsal, rollback, and customer communication, with a clear transition gate. Indefinite delay without these gains is not improved defence. (VI.1-8)

## Key Takeaways

1. Name what defence protects and what time buys.
2. Build layered resistance and recovery.
3. Treat decisive dependencies as hypotheses.
4. Define the transition from preservation to positive action.
