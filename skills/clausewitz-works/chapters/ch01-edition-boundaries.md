# Reference Unit 1: Edition and Boundaries

## Core Idea

This edition combines an unfinished nineteenth-century work with several editorial voices. Reliable use identifies speaker, book, chapter, revision status, and the limits of modern transfer.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Voice Separation**: Distinguish Clausewitz's chapters from Marie von Clausewitz's preface, Graham's translation, and Maude's introduction and notes.
  - When to use: Before citing political, historical, technological, or evaluative claims.
  - How: Locate the text inside the numbered books and verify whether a note or introduction supplies the wording.
- **Revision Boundary**: Treat the work's internal tensions as evidence of incomplete revision rather than force them into a seamless system.
  - When to use: Absolute formulations seem to conflict with limited aims or policy.
  - How: Compare Book I with Book VIII and preserve the distinction between abstract tendency and real war.

## Key Concepts

- **Unfinished work**: Material published after the author's death and not uniformly revised.
- **Editor/annotator**: F. N. Maude, whose later introduction and notes are not Clausewitz.
- **Translator**: J. J. Graham, mediating terminology in this English edition.
- **Locator**: Book and chapter, with subsection where the edition provides one.
- **Transfer limit**: Boundary between military analysis and nonmilitary decision use.

## Mental Models

- Read apparent contradiction as a prompt to inspect level of abstraction and revision history.
- Transfer execution patterns, not violence or adversarial identity.

## Anti-patterns

- **Preface laundering**: Attributing Maude's twentieth-century political claims to Clausewitz.
- **Single-line doctrine**: Building a universal rule from one famous sentence.
- **Militarization by metaphor**: Recasting ordinary cooperation as war.

## Worked Example

When using the relationship between war and policy, retrieve Book VIII, ch. 6B and compare Book I, ch. 1. Do not cite Maude's introduction as evidence of Clausewitz's own political judgment. In a product plan, retain only the structural question—whether means remain subordinate to the governing objective.

## Key Takeaways

1. Cite Book and Chapter.
2. Separate author, translator, editor, and posthumous publisher.
3. Preserve incomplete revision and abstraction levels.
4. Apply nonmilitary analogies narrowly.
