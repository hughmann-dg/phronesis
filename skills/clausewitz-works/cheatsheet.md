# Clausewitz Decision Cheatsheet

## Route the question

| Situation | Load | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| Objective drift or activity overload | ch02, ch09 | What political or governing purpose controls the means? |
| Execution fragility | ch02, ch04-ch06 | Which small impediments compound into failure? |
| Conflicting reports | ch02-ch03 | What is observed, inferred, delayed, or contradicted? |
| Scattered effort | ch04 | Where must effort be concentrated, and what flexibility is real? |
| Defensive posture | ch07 | What does time buy, and when does defence transition? |
| Expansion momentum | ch08 | Where is the culminating point? |
| Whole-plan coherence | ch09 | Are aim, effort, risk, and termination mutually consistent? |

## Decision rules

- If the objective cannot be stated independently of the chosen means, stop and reframe. (I.1; VIII.6)
- If success requires many small things to go right simultaneously, budget friction explicitly. (I.7)
- If reports conflict, calibrate by access, incentive, recency, and corroboration rather than confidence of delivery. (I.6)
- If resources are spread across several supposedly decisive goals, none may be decisive. (III.11-14)
- If advantage declines with each further increment, consolidate before culmination turns success into exposure. (VII.5, VII.22)
- If a defensive posture has no transition condition, it is delay without strategy. (VI.1-5)

## Hard stops

- A coherent strategy cannot legitimize an unlawful or immoral objective.
- Do not apply violent or enemy language to ordinary stakeholders by default.
- Do not treat historical military claims as current operational evidence.
- Do not use centre-of-gravity analysis as a license to target people or civilian systems.
- Retrieve the primary edition and distinguish editorial material before quoting.
