## Core Frameworks and Mental Models

### Keep means subordinate to the political object

War is treated as an instrument serving a political purpose, and the scale and form of effort vary with that purpose and surrounding relations. In nonmilitary use, restate the governing objective and test whether each means advances it without becoming an end in itself. (*On War*, I.1; VIII.6A-B)

### Distinguish abstract tendency from real conduct

Clausewitz develops an abstract interaction that can drive toward extremes, then restores political purpose, probability, human judgment, and actual conditions. Use ideal models to expose forces, not to predict mechanically. (I.1; VIII.2-3)

### Plan for friction

Individually small impediments compound in execution. A plan that works only when communication, timing, effort, weather, morale, and judgment are perfect is not robust. Identify failure paths, slack, rehearsal, reserves, and adaptation triggers. (I.7)

### Discount information without discarding it

Reports in action are incomplete, contradictory, and often wrong. Separate source reliability, timeliness, corroboration, and decision relevance; avoid both credulity and paralysis. (I.6; II.5-6)

### Concentrate effort at the decision

Strategy coordinates engagements toward the objective. Assembly in space and time, economy of force, and reserves concern where commitment is decisive and where flexibility still has value. Avoid scattering resources across goals that cannot all be decisive. (III.1, III.11-14)

### Treat defence and attack as asymmetric

Clausewitz argues that defence has intrinsic advantages while attack carries positive purpose and tends to lose strength as it advances. Do not treat the two as mirror images; compare time, position, support, burden, and the transition between them. (VI.1-5; VII.1-5)

### Watch for culmination

Operational advantage can reach a point after which further advance weakens the attacker and changes the balance. Define stop conditions before momentum, success, and sunk costs hide declining marginal advantage. (VII.5; VII.22)

### Design the whole from the governing purpose

Book VIII connects limited and overthrow aims, magnitude of effort, interdependence, and policy. A coherent plan states the kind of objective, required effort, acceptable risk, and conditions for termination. (VIII.3-6)

## Sources and Limits

Primary edition: J. J. Graham translation, new and revised edition with F. N. Maude's introduction and notes, eighth impression in three volumes, Project Gutenberg #1946 (`clausewitz-on-war`). The transcription notes an 1874 first translation and a 1909 London reprinting but does not establish the exact eighth-impression year. Rights verification is scoped to the United States.

*On War* was unfinished and unevenly revised. Editorial introductions contain later historical and political claims that are not Clausewitz's text. Claims about modern weapons, law, psychology, organizations, states, or current conflict need modern evidence. The skill extracts planning concepts; it never authorizes violence, militarization of ordinary relationships, illegal conduct, or pursuit of an illegitimate objective.
