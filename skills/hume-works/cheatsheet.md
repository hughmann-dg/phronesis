# Hume Evidence Cheatsheet

## Route the claim

| Claim | Load | First question |
|---|---|---|
| Undefined or abstract concept | ch03 | From what experience is this idea derived? |
| Forecast from historical data | ch05-ch07 | What makes the observed cases relevant to the target? |
| Causal explanation | ch05, ch08 | What is observed beyond sequence or correlation? |
| Conflicting reports | ch07, ch11 | How reliable and independent are the witnesses and base rates? |
| Free-will or accountability claim | ch09 | Which meaning of liberty, necessity, and constraint is in use? |
| Analogy from observed effects | ch12 | Does the conclusion exceed what the effects support? |
| Certainty or paralysis | ch13 | What would proportioned, provisional belief permit? |

## Decision rules

- If a claim's contrary is conceivable, do not present it as demonstratively certain. (IV)
- If past cases support a forecast, state the continuity assumption and check for changed conditions. (IV-V)
- If experience is mixed, confidence should reflect contrary cases, not narrative vividness. (VI)
- If a causal story rests only on succession, distinguish observed pattern from necessary power and mechanism. (VII)
- If reports share a source or incentive, do not count them as independent corroboration. (X)
- If skepticism blocks all action, use safeguards, reversibility, and update triggers rather than pretending certainty. (V; XII)

## Hard stops

- Do not use Hume to dismiss current science or domain expertise.
- Do not treat a historical psychology as settled empirical fact.
- Do not use Section X as a prejudice-based shortcut against witnesses or communities.
- Do not flatten stronger and weaker evidence into equal uncertainty.
- Retrieve the source and cite exact Section before quoting.
