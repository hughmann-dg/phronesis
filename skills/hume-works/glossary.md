# Glossary

Definitions synthesize the verified Selby-Bigge edition and are not quotations.

**Association of ideas** - tendency for ideas to follow relations of resemblance, contiguity, and cause/effect. (III)

**Belief** - a more forceful or lively conception connected with a present impression through habit. (V)

**Cause and effect** - relation known through experience of conjunction and resulting inference rather than a directly perceived necessary power. (IV, VII)

**Constant conjunction** - repeated pairing of kinds of events that supports expectation and forms part of Hume's causal definition. (VII, Part II)

**Custom or habit** - mechanism by which repetition produces expectation from one event to another. (V, Part I)

**Idea** - comparatively faint perception in thinking or remembering. (II)

**Impression** - more forceful perception in sensation, emotion, or immediate awareness. (II)

**Induction** - inference from observed cases to unobserved or future cases; the modern label is useful, though Hume frames the problem through matters of fact and experience. (IV)

**Liberty** - in Section VIII, power to act or not act according to the will when not constrained. (VIII, Part I)

**Matter of fact** - proposition whose contrary remains conceivable and whose support depends on evidence. (IV, Part I)

**Mitigated skepticism** - bounded, reflective fallibilism that restrains claims while permitting ordinary reasoning and action. (XII, Part III)

**Necessary connection** - disputed content of causal necessity, analyzed through experienced regularity and mental inference. (VII)

**Probability** - degree of belief shaped by varying or contrary experience. (VI)

**Relation of ideas** - proposition established through conceptual or demonstrative relations rather than contingent experience. (IV, Part I)

**Testimony** - reports from people whose evidential force depends on experience of human veracity and circumstances of the witnesses. (X)
