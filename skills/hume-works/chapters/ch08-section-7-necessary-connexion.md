# Reference Unit 8: Section VII — Necessary Connection

## Core Idea

Hume finds no directly perceived necessary power in external sequences or voluntary action and reconstructs causal necessity through repeated conjunction and the mind's resulting inference.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Power-Concept Audit**: Ask what experiential content supports a claim of power or necessity. (VII, Part I)
  - When to use: A mechanism is asserted but not observed or tested.
  - How: Separate event sequence, intervention, mechanism evidence, and felt expectation.
- **Constant-Conjunction Definition**: State the regular relation and the inference it supports without claiming more necessity than evidence supplies. (VII, Part II)
  - When to use: Writing a causal claim.
  - How: Specify cause class, effect class, conditions, repeated pattern, and exceptions.
- **Causal-Language Calibration**: Match wording to design strength. (VII)
  - When to use: Reports move from correlation to cause.
  - How: Choose associated with, predicts, contributes to, or causes based on evidence.

## Key Concepts

- **Power/efficacy**: Supposed productive connection whose impression is disputed. (VII.1)
- **Necessary connection**: Content analyzed through regularity and inference. (VII)
- **Constant conjunction**: Repeated pairing of relevant event types. (VII.2)
- **Mental determination**: Transition of thought built by custom. (VII.2)
- **Mechanism evidence**: Modern evidential category that must be supplied independently.

## Mental Models

- Treat causal certainty as stronger than observed succession.
- Use Hume to demand clearer evidence, not to deny causal investigation.

## Anti-patterns

- **Post hoc mechanism**: Inventing productive power from sequence alone.
- **Humean nihilism**: Claiming causation can never be studied.

## Worked Example

Hume repeatedly uses collision and motion to show that necessity is not visually perceived in a single event. For an A/B test, observing higher conversion after a treatment is stronger than mere sequence but still requires design integrity, uncertainty analysis, and boundary conditions before broad causal transfer. (VII)

## Key Takeaways

1. Separate sequence, regularity, mechanism, and inference.
2. State causal boundary conditions.
3. Calibrate language to evidence.
4. Do not turn skepticism into denial of empirical methods.
