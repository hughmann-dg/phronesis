# Reference Unit 6: Section V — Sceptical Solution

## Core Idea

Custom or habit, not a new demonstration, explains why repeated experience produces expectation and belief; practical inference remains indispensable while certainty is restrained.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Custom-Based Expectation**: Treat repeated conjunction as producing a graded practical expectation. (V, Part I)
  - When to use: A pattern guides action despite the limits identified in Section IV.
  - How: Measure repetitions, similarity, stability, counterinstances, and decision stakes.
- **Belief-Force Distinction**: Belief differs from a merely imagined possibility partly in how forcefully it is conceived. (V, Part II)
  - When to use: Vividness or familiarity may be mistaken for evidence.
  - How: Separate felt conviction from source quality and diagnostic support.
- **Provisional Action Rule**: Act on well-supported expectation with safeguards and update conditions. (V)
  - When to use: Waiting for certainty is impossible.
  - How: Bound downside, choose reversibility, monitor, and update.

## Key Concepts

- **Custom/habit**: Repetition-driven mechanism of expectation. (V.1)
- **Belief**: Lively conception associated with a present impression. (V.2)
- **Expectation**: Anticipation formed by experienced conjunction.
- **Fiction**: Conceived possibility lacking the force of belief.
- **Practical inference**: Action-guiding expectation without demonstration.

## Mental Models

- Use habit to explain confidence, then evaluate whether the habit remains well calibrated.
- Think of reversibility as a bridge between uncertainty and action.

## Anti-patterns

- **Familiarity equals truth**: Treating repeated exposure as independent confirmation.
- **Skeptical paralysis**: Refusing all practical inference because demonstration is unavailable.

## Worked Example

Hume's account describes expectation arising after repeated conjunction. In operations, a team expects a rollback to resolve a familiar failure because it has repeatedly done so. The team may act, but it checks whether the current symptoms and version match past cases and monitors for divergence. (V)

## Key Takeaways

1. Explain expectation through custom without claiming certainty.
2. Separate conviction from evidential strength.
3. Act provisionally with safeguards.
4. Update when conditions or outcomes diverge.
