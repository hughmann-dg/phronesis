# Reference Unit 11: Section X — Miracles

## Core Idea

Section X compares testimony with background experience and examines witness reliability, contradiction, social transmission, and incentives; it requires contextual evidence weighing, not a slogan-based dismissal of reports.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Testimony Reliability Matrix**: Evaluate access, competence, track record, incentives, consistency, and corroboration. (X, Part I)
  - When to use: Important facts depend on reports.
  - How: Assess each witness and the evidence-generating process.
- **Independence Check**: Multiple reports add less when copied from one source or shaped by a shared incentive. (X, Parts I-II)
  - When to use: Apparent corroboration is numerous.
  - How: Trace provenance, communication, and shared dependencies.
- **Background-Evidence Comparison**: Compare the reliability of the testimony process with the evidence supporting the relevant regularity. (X, Part I)
  - When to use: A report conflicts with well-supported expectations.
  - How: Audit both sides; background beliefs can also be biased or outdated.

## Key Concepts

- **Testimony**: Evidence produced through human reporting.
- **Witness quality**: Access, competence, honesty, and incentives.
- **Contrary proof**: Evidence pulling confidence in opposite directions.
- **Dependence**: Shared source or process reducing corroborative value.
- **Extraordinary claim**: Interpretive label requiring explicit evidential comparison.

## Mental Models

- Treat testimony as generated data with provenance.
- Audit the background model as carefully as the surprising report.

## Anti-patterns

- **Slogan skepticism**: Rejecting a report by label instead of analysis.
- **Witness prejudice**: Discounting people by identity rather than evidential factors.
- **Corroboration counting**: Counting copied reports as independent.

## Worked Example

Several dashboards report a security incident, but all ingest one faulty sensor. Apparent multiplicity is not independent corroboration. Investigators trace provenance, inspect raw logs, test the sensor, and compare the report with known failure modes before updating confidence. (X)

## Key Takeaways

1. Evaluate testimony through provenance and reliability.
2. Check independence before counting corroboration.
3. Compare report evidence with audited background evidence.
4. Never use the section as a prejudice-based shortcut.
