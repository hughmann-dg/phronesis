# Reference Unit 2: Section I — Different Species of Philosophy

## Core Idea

Hume contrasts accessible moral reflection aimed at conduct with abstruse inquiry into the principles of human understanding, then argues for disciplined clarity rather than abandoning difficult investigation.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Use-and-Rigor Pairing**: Join practical relevance with careful inquiry. (I)
  - When to use: Analysis is either rhetorically attractive but shallow or precise but disconnected from action.
  - How: State the practical question, examine the underlying cognitive principle, and return the result to conduct in clear language.
- **Obscurity Audit**: Difficult reasoning deserves scrutiny because error can hide in abstraction. (I)
  - When to use: Jargon or metaphysics shields a claim from testing.
  - How: Define terms, reconstruct steps, identify experience bearing on the claim, and narrow what cannot be established.

## Key Concepts

- **Easy philosophy**: Reflection tied to character, sentiment, and common life.
- **Abstruse philosophy**: Inquiry into underlying principles of understanding.
- **Human nature**: Proposed common subject connecting intellectual fields.
- **Clarity**: Constraint on inquiry, not a substitute for depth.

## Mental Models

- Use depth to improve practice and practice to test relevance.
- Think of obscurity as a risk factor requiring reconstruction, not proof of falsehood.

## Anti-patterns

- **Popular-equals-true**: Treating accessibility as evidence.
- **Difficulty prestige**: Treating opacity as intellectual depth.

## Worked Example

A forecast meeting alternates between motivational anecdotes and an opaque model. The pairing requires a clear decision question, transparent assumptions, and an explanation of how the model changes action. Neither inspiration nor mathematics gets a free pass. (I)

## Key Takeaways

1. Combine practical use with rigorous inquiry.
2. Reconstruct obscure claims before accepting them.
3. Explain how analysis changes conduct.
4. Keep claims within human evidential reach.
