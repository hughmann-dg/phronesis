# Reference Unit 5: Section IV — Sceptical Doubts

## Core Idea

Section IV separates relations of ideas from matters of fact and argues that inference from observed causes and effects to unobserved cases depends on an assumption that reasoning cannot establish demonstratively or by noncircular experience.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Claim-Type Gate**: Separate relations of ideas from matters of fact. (IV, Part I)
  - When to use: A forecast or causal claim is presented as logically certain.
  - How: Ask whether the contrary is conceivable and what experience supports the claim.
- **Inductive-Step Exposure**: Make the move from observed to unobserved cases explicit. (IV, Parts I-II)
  - When to use: Historical regularity supports a prediction.
  - How: State the observed sample, target, continuity assumption, and possible change in conditions.
- **Circularity Check**: Do not justify reliance on experience solely by saying it worked in past experience. (IV, Part II)
  - When to use: A method's track record is treated as complete proof of future reliability.
  - How: Add mechanism, stability, external validation, and bounded action.

## Key Concepts

- **Relation of ideas**: Demonstrative or conceptual proposition. (IV.1)
- **Matter of fact**: Contingent proposition supported through evidence. (IV.1)
- **Cause-effect inference**: Passage beyond memory and sense. (IV.1)
- **Uniformity assumption**: Expectation that relevant regularities continue.
- **Circular justification**: Using the reliability under question to prove itself.

## Mental Models

- Use conceivability of the contrary to calibrate certainty claims.
- Treat every forecast as carrying a transport assumption from sample to target.

## Anti-patterns

- **Forecast-as-theorem**: Presenting a contingent estimate as demonstration.
- **Past-performance tautology**: Saying the future will resemble the past because it has before.

## Worked Example

Hume uses ordinary expectations such as nourishment from familiar food to show how quickly thought moves from past experience to future effect. A product forecast likewise moves from prior cohorts to a new cohort; the audit names changes in price, channel, population, and environment rather than hiding the transfer. (IV)

## Key Takeaways

1. Classify propositions before evaluating proof.
2. State the observed-to-unobserved step.
3. Expose continuity assumptions.
4. Replace certainty with calibrated, testable expectation.
