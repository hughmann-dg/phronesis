# Reference Unit 12: Section XI — Particular Providence and Future State

## Core Idea

Through a staged dialogue, Section XI warns against inferring more about a cause than observed effects support and against using a speculative cause to add unsupported consequences back into experience.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Effect-Bounded Inference**: Do not attribute more to a cause than is needed to account for observed effects. (XI)
  - When to use: Inferring hidden capability, intention, or design from an outcome.
  - How: List observed effects, minimum explanatory commitments, alternatives, and residual uncertainty.
- **No-Reimport Rule**: Do not infer a cause from effects and then use the enriched cause to predict new effects without independent support. (XI)
  - When to use: A speculative model generates confident downstream claims.
  - How: Mark which properties came from data and which were added by theory.
- **Analogy-Proportion Test**: Match conclusions to the strength and closeness of analogy. (XI)
  - When to use: Reasoning from human artifacts or motives to a larger unseen cause.
  - How: Compare relevant structure and disanalogies.

## Key Concepts

- **Inference from effects**: Reasoning backward to a cause.
- **Proportioned cause**: Cause specified no more strongly than effects warrant.
- **Speculative enrichment**: Adding unsupported properties to an inferred cause.
- **Reimported consequence**: Prediction derived from those added properties.
- **Analogy**: Bounded similarity used in inference.

## Mental Models

- Use minimum sufficient explanation before richer stories.
- Track which model properties were observed and which were assumed.

## Anti-patterns

- **Intent from outcome**: Treating a result as proof of a specific motive.
- **Model self-confirmation**: Using assumptions added by a theory as new evidence for it.

## Worked Example

A competitor's price cut is observed. The effect supports several possible causes: excess inventory, market entry, cost reduction, or tactical pressure. Inferring an unlimited cash reserve and then predicting years of cuts reimports unsupported properties. The team gathers financial and market evidence before acting. (XI)

## Key Takeaways

1. Bound inferred causes by observed effects.
2. Separate data-derived properties from theoretical additions.
3. Do not generate new certainty from enriched speculation.
4. Test analogies for relevant similarity.
