# Reference Unit 1: Edition and Boundaries

## Core Idea

The source is Selby-Bigge's 1902 second edition of Hume's *Enquiry*, reprinted from the 1777 posthumous edition and extracted from a larger volume; editorial apparatus and Hume's numbered text must remain distinct.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Section-Locator Rule**: Cite the displayed Section and, for exact wording, the edition's numbered paragraph.
  - When to use: Every important doctrinal claim or quotation.
  - How: Retrieve `hume-enquiry`, locate the numbered section and paragraph, and confirm the passage in context.
- **Apparatus Boundary**: Treat contents, comparative tables, and analytical index as Selby-Bigge's editorial aids.
  - When to use: A useful category or phrase appears outside the numbered enquiry.
  - How: Attribute it to the editor or use it only as a navigation aid.

## Key Concepts

- **1777 text**: Posthumous edition from which the enquiry is reprinted.
- **1902 second edition**: Selby-Bigge's edited source artifact.
- **Editorial apparatus**: Introduction, tables, and index surrounding Hume's text.
- **Numbered paragraph**: Edition-specific finer locator within a Section.
- **Interpretation**: Synthesis linked to, but not identical with, primary wording.

## Mental Models

- Use an index to find evidence, never as the evidence itself.
- Treat familiar labels such as induction as navigation terms when Hume's own wording differs.

## Anti-patterns

- **Index attribution**: Presenting Selby-Bigge's category as Hume's sentence.
- **Modern-term projection**: Assuming later philosophical vocabulary occurs in the source.
- **Quotation by memory**: Reconstructing famous phrases without retrieval.

## Worked Example

To support a claim about causation, use the analytical index only to locate Section VII, then retrieve the numbered text and cite the relevant part and paragraph. The summary in ch08 may guide interpretation but must not be quoted as Hume.

## Key Takeaways

1. Cite Section and exact edition.
2. Distinguish Hume from Selby-Bigge's apparatus.
3. Retrieve before quoting.
4. Mark modern terminology as interpretive when necessary.
