# Reference Unit 4: Section III — Association of Ideas

## Core Idea

Hume identifies resemblance, contiguity, and cause/effect as recurring relations by which thought passes from one idea to another.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Association Trace**: Identify what relation moved thought from evidence to conclusion. (III)
  - When to use: A narrative feels coherent before its inferential steps are clear.
  - How: Mark resemblance, spatial or temporal contiguity, asserted causation, and any unstated transition.
- **Narrative-Coherence Audit**: Distinguish psychological flow from evidential support. (III and the section's discussion of composition)
  - When to use: A story's sequence creates conviction.
  - How: Reconstruct the association, then test each factual and causal link independently.

## Key Concepts

- **Resemblance**: Similarity calling one idea to mind from another.
- **Contiguity**: Nearness in place or time.
- **Cause and effect**: Experienced relation that becomes a strong associative principle.
- **Imagination**: Faculty connecting and recombining ideas.
- **Coherence**: Order of thought that may or may not equal proof.

## Mental Models

- Treat narrative fluency as a psychological fact requiring evidential review.
- Use association labels to expose hidden transitions in an argument.

## Anti-patterns

- **Story-as-proof**: Treating smooth narrative sequence as causal evidence.
- **Similarity inference**: Assuming two similar cases share the decisive mechanism.

## Worked Example

A company failure is narrated as product launch, negative press, customer churn, and executive departure. Contiguity makes the causal story compelling, but the audit checks timing, segment data, prior churn, competitor actions, and alternative explanations before assigning cause. (III)

## Key Takeaways

1. Name the relation carrying each transition.
2. Separate psychological coherence from evidence.
3. Test resemblance and sequence for mechanism and alternatives.
4. Use the trace as preparation for causal audit.
