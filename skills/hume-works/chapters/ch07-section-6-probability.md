# Reference Unit 7: Section VI — Probability

## Core Idea

Where chances or experience vary, judgment reflects competing possibilities and the balance of observed outcomes rather than certainty.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Contrary-Case Balance**: Count and weight supporting and opposing experience. (VI)
  - When to use: Evidence is mixed.
  - How: Define comparable cases, record outcomes, assess relevance, and show uncertainty.
- **Chance Decomposition**: Enumerate possible outcomes instead of reasoning from a single vivid scenario. (VI)
  - When to use: Rare but salient outcomes dominate attention.
  - How: List outcomes, base rates, conditional evidence, and decision consequences.
- **Experience-Uniformity Scale**: Confidence increases with uniform, relevant experience and decreases with variation. (VI)
  - When to use: Calibrating a recurring operational or behavioral claim.
  - How: Inspect sample size, comparability, selection, and contrary instances.

## Key Concepts

- **Probability of chances**: Belief divided among possible outcomes. (VI)
- **Probability of causes**: Confidence shaped by variable experience of effects. (VI)
- **Contrary experiments**: Observations supporting different outcomes.
- **Uniform experience**: Repeated, relevant observations without known counterinstances.
- **Calibration**: Matching confidence to evidential balance.

## Mental Models

- Use contrary cases as information, not anomalies to delete.
- Distinguish probability of occurrence from severity of consequence.

## Anti-patterns

- **Anecdote dominance**: Letting one vivid case outweigh a relevant distribution.
- **Silent denominator**: Reporting successes without the comparable opportunities.

## Worked Example

Hume discusses the mind's distribution across contrary possibilities. For a release estimate, twelve similar changes succeeded and three failed under identifiable load conditions. The forecast reports the mixed record, conditions on load, and plans a canary rather than announcing either certainty or ignorance. (VI)

## Key Takeaways

1. Include contrary evidence.
2. Make denominators and comparability visible.
3. Separate likelihood from impact.
4. Express confidence as graded.
