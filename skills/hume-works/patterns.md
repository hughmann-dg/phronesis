# Patterns

## Claim-Type Gate

**When to use**: A disagreement mixes definitions, deductions, observations, forecasts, and values.

**How**:
1. Classify the claim as relation of ideas, observed matter of fact, inferred matter of fact, estimate, opinion, or value.
2. Match the evidence standard to the type.
3. Expose assumptions connecting evidence to conclusion.
4. Record what would change confidence.

**Trade-offs**: Classification clarifies proof burdens but does not settle normative disagreement. (IV, Part I)

## Inductive Extrapolation Audit

**When to use**: Past performance is used to predict a new period, population, or context.

**How**:
1. Define observed sample and target case.
2. State the assumed continuity between them.
3. Compare mechanism, context, selection, and time stability.
4. Seek counterinstances and distribution shift.
5. Use ranges, reversible trials, and update triggers.

**Trade-offs**: Action cannot wait for certainty; the goal is visible, calibrated extrapolation. (IV-V)

## Causal-Story Audit

**When to use**: A compelling narrative is offered as causal proof.

**How**:
1. Separate sequence, correlation, mechanism evidence, intervention evidence, and alternative causes.
2. Identify repeated conjunction and boundary conditions.
3. Test whether the cause predicts novel cases.
4. Calibrate language to the evidence.

**Trade-offs**: Hume's analysis does not forbid causal science; it disciplines what evidence supports the claim. (VII)

## Testimony Reliability Matrix

**When to use**: Important facts arrive through witnesses or reports.

**How**:
1. Evaluate access, competence, incentives, track record, and internal consistency.
2. Check independence among reports.
3. Compare with background evidence and known error processes.
4. Seek direct records or tests where proportionate.
5. Update rather than decide by slogan.

**Trade-offs**: Background expectations can encode bias; audit the background evidence too. (X)

## Mitigated-Skeptic Action Rule

**When to use**: Uncertainty tempts either dogmatism or paralysis.

**How**:
1. State current evidence and confidence.
2. Bound downside with reversibility and safeguards.
3. Gather the highest-value evidence available in time.
4. Act provisionally and define update triggers.

**Trade-offs**: Provisional action accepts residual risk; delay also has consequences. (V; XII, Part III)
