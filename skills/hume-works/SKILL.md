## Core Frameworks and Mental Models

### Separate impressions from ideas

Hume distinguishes more forceful perceptions from fainter ideas and uses the copy principle as a test of disputed concepts: ask from what impression an idea is derived. Use this as a concept audit, while preserving the exceptions and complications the text itself acknowledges. (*Enquiry*, II)

### Classify propositions before testing them

Relations of ideas are established through the relations among concepts; matters of fact concern what could be otherwise and depend on evidence. Do not demand empirical proof for a definition or treat a factual forecast as demonstratively certain. (IV, Part I)

### Expose the inductive step

Reasoning from observed to unobserved cases depends on assuming some continuity between past and future. Hume argues that this step is not established by demonstration or by a noncircular appeal to experience. Make the extrapolation visible instead of laundering it as fact. (IV, Parts I-II)

### Treat custom as the mechanism of expectation

Repeated conjunction produces expectation and belief through custom or habit. This explains practical inference without converting it into certainty. Ask how much repetition, similarity, stability, and counterevidence support the expectation. (V, Part I)

### Calibrate probability by contrary evidence

Where experience varies, confidence should reflect the balance and uniformity of observations rather than the vividness of one story. Use base rates, sample relevance, counterinstances, and uncertainty ranges. (VI)

### Define causal content modestly

Experience reveals repeated succession and conjunction, not an observed necessary power binding events. Causal language should identify the pattern, mechanism evidence where available, and the conditions under which the relation holds. (VII, Parts I-II)

### Reconcile regularity with accountable action carefully

Section VIII frames necessity through regularity and inference while defining liberty in terms of acting according to one's will when unconstrained. Modern responsibility questions require legal and moral frameworks beyond this historical argument. (VIII, Parts I-II)

### Weight testimony against background experience

Testimony is evidence whose force depends on witness reliability, number, independence, incentives, consistency, and fit with well-supported experience. Extraordinary claims require correspondingly strong evidence, but the analysis must not become a slogan that dismisses evidence without examination. (X, Parts I-II)

### Practice mitigated skepticism

Section XII rejects both dogmatic certainty and total suspension of ordinary reasoning. Restrict inquiry to capacities and evidence available to human understanding, proportion belief to evidence, and continue acting under acknowledged fallibility. (XII, Parts I-III)
