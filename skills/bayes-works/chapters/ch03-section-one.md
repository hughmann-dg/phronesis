# Reference Unit 3: Section I — Chance Foundations

## Core Idea

Section I defines chance and expectation and develops propositions for contrary events and repeated trials, supplying forward-probability foundations used by the inverse construction.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Event Algebra Check**: Define events, contraries, and compound conditions before arithmetic. (Section I, pp. 376-389)
  - When to use: Probabilities overlap or omit cases.
  - How: Make events mutually clear and state whether they are exhaustive.
- **Dependence Check**: Confirm whether repeated trial probabilities are modeled as stable and independent.
  - When to use: Binomial counts summarize time, people, or clustered systems.
  - How: Look for shared causes, drift, feedback, and selection.
- **Expectation Unit Check**: State what gain/loss unit is being averaged. (early definitions)

## Key Concepts

- **Chance**: Ratio-based measure of expectation in Bayes's definitions.
- **Expectation**: Chance-linked valuation of gain or loss.
- **Contrary event**: Complementary alternative in the early propositions.
- **Repeated trials**: Sequence used to derive event-count chances.

## Anti-patterns

- **Probability without event**: Giving a number before defining what happens.
- **Independence by convenience**: Using a binomial model because it is easy.
- **Unit mixing**: Combining monetary, safety, and dignity outcomes as one expectation without justification.

## Worked Example

Customer churn observations share the same price change and season, so they are not 500 independent pieces of evidence about a stable rate. The model introduces cohort and time effects before updating.

## Key Takeaways

1. Define event and contrary.
2. State the sampling structure.
3. Test stability and independence.
4. Keep expectation units explicit.
