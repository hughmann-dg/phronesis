# Glossary — Bayes's Essay

**Chance** — Bayes's term for a ratio expressing expectation about an event under specified circumstances (Section I, definitions, pp. 376-377).

**Contrary events** — event and non-event paired in the early propositions (Section I, pp. 377-380).

**Expectation** — value concept defined from chance and what is gained or lost; historical usage should not be silently equated with every modern expected-utility model (Section I, pp. 376-377).

**Independent trials** — repeated trials whose event chances are modeled without dependence; modern applications must test this assumption (Section I).

**Inverse probability** — later label for inferring an unknown chance from observed outcomes; the paper frames this as its central problem (Problem, pp. 375-376).

**Likelihood** — modern term for probability of observed evidence under a hypothesized parameter; useful for interpretation but not Bayes's own vocabulary here.

**Posterior** — modern term for an updated probability distribution after conditioning on evidence; not the paper's word.

**Postulate** — Section II construction that gives the unknown chance a uniform geometrical basis (pp. 389-390).

**Prior** — modern term for probability assigned before the reported observations; label it as later vocabulary when discussing Bayes.

**Proposition 9** — central inverse result relating observed event counts to probability that the underlying chance lies between bounds (pp. 395-399).

**Scholium** — application and approximation discussion following Proposition 9 (pp. 399-418).
