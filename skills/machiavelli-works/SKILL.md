## Core Frameworks and Mental Models

### Start from conduct under actual constraints

Machiavelli contrasts analysis of how rulers and peoples act with idealized political instruction. Use the lens to expose incentive, dependence, coercive capacity, and reputation effects; do not infer that whatever succeeds is morally permitted. (*Prince*, ch. XV)

### Distinguish modes of acquisition

Inherited rule, mixed acquisition, civic elevation, personal capacity, fortune, and wrongdoing create different dependencies and stability problems. Diagnose how a position was acquired before forecasting how it can be kept. (*Prince*, chs. I-IX)

### Prefer capabilities one can command

Mercenary and auxiliary forces create agency and loyalty problems because their incentives and command do not reliably coincide with the ruler's objective. Translate the insight into modern dependency analysis: identify critical capabilities controlled by vendors, sponsors, or borrowed authority. (*Prince*, chs. XII-XIV)

### Avoid hatred and contempt

Fear, reputation, property, promises, and visible conduct interact. Machiavelli repeatedly treats hatred and contempt as destabilizing constraints, not as feelings a decision-maker can ignore. Test whether an option creates concentrated grievance, signals weakness, or destroys credible commitment. (*Prince*, chs. XVII-XIX)

### Match conduct to changing times

Fortune supplies conditions that cannot be commanded, while different dispositions fit different periods. Do not confuse a strategy that worked under one tempo or coalition with a universal rule; compare the actor's habitual style with the demands of the moment. (*Prince*, ch. XXV; *Discourses*, III.9)

### Treat institutions as channels for conflict

The *Discourses* argues that durable republics do not eliminate social conflict; they give competing orders lawful means to accuse, contest, and protect liberty. Diagnose whether disagreement has a legitimate channel or will reappear as intrigue, withdrawal, or force. (*Discourses*, I.4-8)

### Separate founding from maintenance

Concentrated authority may found or refound an order, but maintenance requires laws, offices, succession, correction, and public habits. A heroic launch without durable institutions is fragile. (*Discourses*, I.9-10, I.18; III.1)

### Renew by returning to first principles

Organizations decay as practices drift from their animating purpose. Periodic correction can come through institutions, exemplary conduct, or crisis, but renewal itself can become abusive unless constrained by law and rights. (*Discourses*, III.1)

## Sources and Limits

Primary editions: W. K. Marriott's *The Prince*, Project Gutenberg #1232 (`machiavelli-prince`), and Ninian Hill Thomson's 1883 *Discourses on the First Decade of Titus Livius*, Project Gutenberg #10827 (`machiavelli-discourses`). Rights verification is scoped to the United States.

The Marriott file also contains other Machiavelli works after *The Prince*; they are not silently attributed to its numbered chapters. Thomson's historical examples and both translations' vocabulary require edition-aware citation. Claims involving motive, military effectiveness, mass behavior, gender, religion, ethnicity, or national character require independent modern evidence. This skill supports descriptive political analysis only; legality, rights, safety, truthfulness, and moral legitimacy remain independent constraints.
