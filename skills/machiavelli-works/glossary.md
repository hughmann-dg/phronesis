# Glossary

Definitions are interpretations of the verified editions, not quotations.

**Auxiliary arms** - forces supplied and controlled by another power; effective deployment can deepen dependence on that power. (*Prince*, ch. XIII)

**Calumny** - accusation without an accountable public procedure; Machiavelli contrasts it with lawful accusation. (*Discourses*, I.7-8)

**Civic principality** - rule obtained through support of fellow citizens rather than foreign force or inherited title. (*Prince*, ch. IX)

**Corruption** - decay of civic habits and institutions such that ordinary laws no longer sustain a free order. (*Discourses*, I.17-18)

**Effectual analysis** - attention to conduct and consequences under actual conditions rather than reliance on an imagined ideal order. (*Prince*, ch. XV)

**Fortune** - contingency and changing circumstance that condition but do not wholly determine action. (*Prince*, ch. XXV; *Discourses*, III.9)

**Fortress** - both a literal defense and a recurring example of technical protection that may fail when political support is absent. (*Prince*, ch. XX; *Discourses*, II.24)

**Founding** - creation or radical refounding of an order, distinguished from its long-term maintenance through laws and offices. (*Discourses*, I.9-10)

**Hatred and contempt** - politically destabilizing reactions treated as constraints on durable rule. (*Prince*, chs. XVII-XIX)

**Mercenary arms** - hired forces whose incentives, discipline, and loyalty may diverge from the principal's objective. (*Prince*, ch. XII)

**Mixed principality** - newly acquired territory joined to an existing rule, producing expectations, grievances, and administrative challenges. (*Prince*, ch. III)

**Necessity** - conditions that sharply constrain available conduct; it is an analytical category, not automatic moral permission. (*Discourses*, I.51; II.12)

**Orders** - social or political groups with distinct interests, especially elites and the people. (*Prince*, ch. IX; *Discourses*, I.4-5)

**Own arms** - capabilities whose command, incentives, and maintenance belong to the actor or polity using them. (*Prince*, chs. XII-XIV)

**Renewal** - correction of institutional drift by returning to an order's animating principles. (*Discourses*, III.1)

**Virtu / capacity** - practical ability, judgment, initiative, and force of character in meeting circumstance; no single English moral term captures every use. (*Prince*, chs. VI-VII, XXV)
