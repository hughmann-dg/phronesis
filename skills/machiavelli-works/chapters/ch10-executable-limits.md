# Reference Unit 10: Executable Limits

## Core Idea

Machiavelli's texts can improve descriptive political diagnosis without deciding what is lawful, truthful, safe, or morally legitimate.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Description-Permission Firewall**: Separate a prediction about effectiveness from authorization to act.
  - When to use: A passage concerns deception, coercion, violence, exemplary punishment, emergency power, or manipulation.
  - How: State the descriptive claim, verify evidence, then apply independent law, rights, safety, truthfulness, and moral constraints.
- **Motive-Evidence Gate**: Treat stakeholder motive as a hypothesis unless supported by conduct and incentives.
  - When to use: Analysis risks becoming cynical mind-reading.
  - How: Separate observed action, stated reason, inferred incentive, alternative explanation, and confidence.
- **Historical-Transfer Test**: Ancient and Renaissance examples require translation across institutions and norms.
  - When to use: A political or military example is applied to business or personal life.
  - How: Preserve the abstract dependency or institutional pattern while rejecting obsolete hierarchy, violence, and rights assumptions.

## Key Concepts

- **Independent constraint**: A rule or right not overridden by strategic advantage.
- **Deferral**: Handing a question to legal, safety, ethical, or subject-matter expertise.
- **Evidence calibration**: Matching confidence about motives and effects to available evidence.
- **Analogy boundary**: The point beyond which a historical comparison stops supporting action.

## Mental Models

- Think of strategic analysis as one column in a decision record, not the final vote.
- Use the red team to attack feasibility and hidden incentives; it still does not vote.

## Anti-patterns

- **Machiavellian permission slip**: Treating the author's name as justification.
- **Universal selfishness**: Assuming all actors maximize narrow personal power.
- **Violent metaphor drift**: Turning colleagues, customers, or citizens into enemies by default.

## Worked Example

A manager believes secretly misleading employees will prevent resistance. The lens may predict short-term compliance and later credibility loss, but the firewall rejects the move on truthfulness and rights grounds. The analysis then searches for transparent sequencing, participation, and safeguards that address the same implementation risk.

## Key Takeaways

1. Effectiveness is not permission.
2. Infer motives cautiously and expose alternatives.
3. Transfer structures, not obsolete norms.
4. Defer when legality, safety, or rights are unresolved.
