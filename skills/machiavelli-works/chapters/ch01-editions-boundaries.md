# Reference Unit 1: Editions and Boundaries

## Core Idea

Machiavelli reaches this skill through two different English editions. Sound use identifies the work, translator, internal locator, and paratext before drawing a claim.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Edition Boundary**: Treat Marriott's *Prince* and Thomson's *Discourses* as distinct translated artifacts.
  - When to use: Before quoting or comparing recurring terms such as fortune, necessity, arms, people, or prince.
  - How: Retrieve by source ID, locate the numbered chapter, and attribute introductions or notes to the editor or translator rather than Machiavelli.
- **Work Boundary**: Do not treat *The Prince* as the whole doctrine.
  - When to use: When a claim about rulers, republics, conflict, or institutions seems categorical.
  - How: Compare the relevant *Prince* passage with the *Discourses* before generalizing.

## Key Concepts

- **Primary source**: The immutable rights-verified local text.
- **Interpretation**: The source-linked synthesis in this skill.
- **Behavior**: The bounded procedure used by Machiavellian counsel.
- **Paratext**: Introductions, notes, and appended material surrounding the attributed work.
- **Locator**: Work plus book and chapter, not a paragraph number invented from a summary.

## Mental Models

- Think of a translation as evidence with provenance, not transparent access to an unmediated original.
- Use cross-work comparison when one passage would otherwise support an extreme conclusion.

## Anti-patterns

- **Machiavelli says** without a work and chapter: It hides edition and context.
- **Historical cosplay**: It substitutes a persona for analysis.
- **Success equals permission**: It collapses descriptive and normative layers.

## Worked Example

If counsel considers breaking a promise, retrieve Marriott's *Prince*, ch. XVIII, then compare the constraint against hatred and contempt in ch. XIX and institutional claims in Thomson's *Discourses*. The retrieval establishes what the edition says; separate legal and moral review determines what may be done.

## Key Takeaways

1. Cite work and locator for important claims.
2. Separate primary text, interpretation, and executable behavior.
3. Compare *The Prince* with the *Discourses* before generalizing.
4. Never attribute paratext silently to Machiavelli.
