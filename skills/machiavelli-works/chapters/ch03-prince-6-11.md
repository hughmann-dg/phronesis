# Reference Unit 3: The Prince, Chapters VI-XI

## Core Idea

New authority is conditioned by the founder's capability, dependence on fortune or sponsors, the means used to acquire power, and the social group supplying support.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Capacity-versus-Fortune Matrix**: Separate what the actor prepared and controls from what favorable circumstance supplied. (*Prince*, chs. VI-VII)
  - When to use: A rapid rise follows timing, sponsorship, crisis, or unusual access.
  - How: Inventory personal or organizational capabilities, borrowed resources, sponsor interests, and what fails when conditions normalize.
- **Civic-Support Test**: Rule supported by elites and rule supported by the people face different expectations and blocking structures. (*Prince*, ch. IX)
  - When to use: A leader depends on a board, executive coalition, workforce, electorate, or customer community.
  - How: Identify the supporting order, its objective, its ability to coordinate, and the grievances of the excluded order.

## Key Concepts

- **New principality**: Authority without established inheritance. (chs. VI-VII)
- **Fortune**: Conditions and opportunities not created by the actor. (ch. VII)
- **Capacity**: The ability to organize, decide, and sustain action. (chs. VI-VII)
- **Wicked acquisition**: Power obtained through grave wrongdoing; Machiavelli distinguishes acquisition from glory. (ch. VIII)
- **Civic support**: Elevation through fellow citizens and social orders. (ch. IX)

## Mental Models

- Use sponsor withdrawal as a stress test for apparently strong authority.
- Separate effectiveness, durability, glory, legality, and morality; they are not synonyms.

## Anti-patterns

- **Outcome laundering**: Treating successful acquisition as moral vindication.
- **Sponsor blindness**: Ignoring the continuing leverage of those who enabled the rise.

## Worked Example

The edition uses Cesare Borgia as a case of authority enabled by another's arms and fortune, followed by efforts to build independent foundations. The analytical lesson is dependency reduction under a closing window; it is not approval of Borgia's violence. In a safe organizational analogue, a sponsored program must establish lawful governance, internal capability, and broad support before the sponsor leaves. (*Prince*, ch. VII)

## Key Takeaways

1. Distinguish acquired position from durable capability.
2. Map sponsor leverage explicitly.
3. Identify whether elite or popular support carries the order.
4. Keep effectiveness separate from ethical permission.
