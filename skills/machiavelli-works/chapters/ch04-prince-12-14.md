# Reference Unit 4: The Prince, Chapters XII-XIV

## Core Idea

Critical capability is fragile when the actor neither commands nor sustains the people and systems on which execution depends.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Own-Arms Test**: Prefer capabilities whose incentives, command, training, and maintenance align with the objective. (*Prince*, chs. XII-XIV)
  - When to use: Strategy depends on vendors, contractors, sponsors, borrowed teams, or opaque platforms.
  - How: Map who recruits, pays, directs, can withdraw, owns knowledge, and bears downside.
- **Auxiliary Trap**: Help controlled by another powerful actor can make success itself a source of dependence. (*Prince*, ch. XIII)
  - When to use: A partner offers decisive capacity but retains command and exit rights.
  - How: Model failure if the partner loses, wins, changes terms, or becomes a competitor.

## Key Concepts

- **Mercenary arms**: Hired capability with weak alignment and commitment. (ch. XII)
- **Auxiliary arms**: Another power's capability placed at one's disposal. (ch. XIII)
- **Own arms**: Capability organized under one's own accountable command. (chs. XII-XIV)
- **Preparation**: Continued study and practice before crisis. (ch. XIV)

## Mental Models

- Think of vendor concentration as borrowed arms when switching is slow and knowledge is external.
- Use success-dependence, not only failure-dependence, in partner risk analysis.

## Anti-patterns

- **Logo ownership**: Calling a capability internal because a contract says so.
- **Single-scenario vendor risk**: Testing only partner failure, not partner leverage after success.

## Worked Example

Machiavelli contrasts hired and borrowed forces with forces a ruler can command and prepare. Reconstructed for a migration: a company outsources architecture, operations, and incident response to one vendor. Even a successful launch leaves the company unable to diagnose or exit. A safer plan retains internal technical ownership, rehearsal, documentation, and a tested fallback. (*Prince*, chs. XII-XIV)

## Key Takeaways

1. Map control, incentives, knowledge, and exit for every critical capability.
2. Test dependence after both failure and success.
3. Build internal judgment even when execution is outsourced.
4. Treat preparation as a continuous strategic responsibility.
