# Reference Unit 2: The Prince, Chapters I-V

## Core Idea

The opening classification links the stability problem to the way rule was acquired and to the prior political habits of the governed.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Acquisition-Type Diagnosis**: Inherited, mixed, and newly acquired positions carry different risks. (*Prince*, chs. I-III)
  - When to use: A leader inherits, merges, purchases, or newly creates authority.
  - How: Identify the prior order, expectations created by acquisition, displaced beneficiaries, and continuing dependencies.
- **Prior-Law Constraint**: Communities accustomed to their own laws retain memories and coordination resources. (*Prince*, ch. V)
  - When to use: Integrating an autonomous team, institution, or jurisdiction.
  - How: Map old rules, identity, leaders, and credible sources of renewed resistance.

## Key Concepts

- **Hereditary principality**: Long-established rule with inherited expectations. (ch. II)
- **Mixed principality**: New acquisition added to an existing domain. (ch. III)
- **Formerly free city**: An acquired community with remembered laws and self-government. (ch. V)
- **Innovation burden**: Losses and disappointments created when a new order replaces the old.

## Mental Models

- Use acquisition history when a title says little about actual legitimacy or control.
- Think of old institutions as stored coordination capacity, not obsolete paperwork.

## Anti-patterns

- **Formal-control fallacy**: Assuming legal acquisition produces immediate loyalty.
- **Blank-slate integration**: Ignoring prior laws, identities, and expectations.

## Worked Example

Machiavelli compares rulers taking culturally similar and dissimilar territories and emphasizes the problems created by changed expectations and retained customs. Reconstructed for an organizational merger: the buyer gains formal authority on day one, while the acquired team's operating norms, informal leaders, and grievance networks remain. Integration planning begins from those continuities rather than the org chart. (*Prince*, chs. III-V)

## Key Takeaways

1. Classify the acquisition before selecting a maintenance strategy.
2. Identify who expected to benefit from change and who lost position.
3. Treat prior laws and identity as active political facts.
4. Do not equate possession with durable control.
