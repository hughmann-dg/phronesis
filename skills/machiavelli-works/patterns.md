# Patterns

## Acquisition-Dependency Audit

**When to use**: A role, market, project, or coalition was obtained through sponsorship, merger, emergency authority, or outside resources.

**How**:
1. Classify how control was acquired.
2. List inherited expectations and displaced interests.
3. Identify capabilities controlled by the acquirer, allies, vendors, and incumbents.
4. Forecast which dependencies persist after formal approval.
5. Build lawful exit, substitution, and accountability paths.

**Trade-offs**: Reducing dependence can cost speed and goodwill. The pattern diagnoses fragility; it does not justify coercion. (*Prince*, chs. III, VI-IX, XII-XIII)

## Hatred-and-Contempt Test

**When to use**: An option is formally possible but politically brittle.

**How**:
1. Identify concentrated losses, humiliation, arbitrary treatment, and broken expectations.
2. Separate fear of enforcement from hatred of the actor and contempt for weakness or inconsistency.
3. Test whether affected groups can coordinate, block, exit, or retaliate.
4. Redesign process, safeguards, explanation, and burden-sharing.

**Trade-offs**: Reputation management cannot replace justice or substantive repair. (*Prince*, chs. XVII-XIX)

## Lawful Conflict Channel

**When to use**: Persistent disagreement is being suppressed or displaced into rumor and intrigue.

**How**:
1. Name the interests and orders in conflict.
2. Provide an accountable forum for accusation, evidence, answer, and remedy.
3. Protect dissent from retaliation and protect respondents from unsupported calumny.
4. Feed recurring disputes into institutional revision.

**Trade-offs**: Open contest can be noisy, but unchanneled conflict becomes less observable and harder to correct. (*Discourses*, I.4-8)

## Return-to-Principles Review

**When to use**: An organization preserves forms while drifting from purpose.

**How**:
1. State the founding purpose and current public commitments.
2. Identify practices that now contradict them.
3. Use regular institutions rather than personal exception where possible.
4. Correct incentives, offices, and review cycles.
5. Recheck rights and legitimacy before invoking emergency renewal.

**Trade-offs**: Appeals to origins can become selective mythology; verify the actual record and present obligations. (*Discourses*, III.1)

## Times-and-Temperament Fit

**When to use**: A previously successful leader or strategy is failing under changed conditions.

**How**:
1. Describe the habitual operating style.
2. Describe the present tempo, coalition, and uncertainty.
3. Identify where habit and circumstance mismatch.
4. Add a complementary leader, reversible trial, or explicit adaptation trigger.

**Trade-offs**: Adaptation without stable principles becomes opportunism. (*Prince*, ch. XXV; *Discourses*, III.9)
