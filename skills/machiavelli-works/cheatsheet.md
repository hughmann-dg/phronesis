# Machiavelli Decision Cheatsheet

## Route the question

| Situation | Load | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| New authority or acquisition | ch02-ch03 | How was control obtained, and whose support remains necessary? |
| Vendor, sponsor, or borrowed capability | ch04 | Who commands the capability when interests diverge? |
| Reputation or resistance risk | ch05-ch06 | Will this create hatred, contempt, grievance, or a blocking coalition? |
| Institutional conflict | ch07 | Is there a lawful channel for accusation, answer, and correction? |
| Expansion or alliance | ch08 | Are capability and grievance growing faster than control? |
| Drift, crisis, or leadership mismatch | ch09 | What must be renewed, and does the operating style fit the times? |

## Decision rules

- If acquisition created dependence, do not treat formal control as operational control. (*Prince*, III, VI-VII)
- If a critical capability is controlled by an ally or vendor, create substitution and exit paths before relying on it. (*Prince*, XII-XIII)
- If a plan concentrates losses while denying voice, expect resistance to move outside formal channels. (*Discourses*, I.4-8)
- If enforcement produces hatred or contempt, technical security alone is a weak remedy. (*Prince*, XVII-XX)
- If a strategy worked only under one tempo or coalition, set adaptation triggers rather than universalizing it. (*Prince*, XXV; *Discourses*, III.9)
- If an organization has drifted, prefer lawful institutional renewal to personal exception. (*Discourses*, I.18; III.1)

## Hard stops

- Descriptive effectiveness never settles legality or morality.
- Do not infer hidden motives without behavioral evidence.
- Do not recommend deceit, coercion, retaliation, or rights violations as default tactics.
- Ancient claims about peoples, classes, religion, gender, or war require independent modern evidence.
- Retrieve the primary edition before quoting; cite exact work and chapter.
